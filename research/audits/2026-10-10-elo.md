## THE FIT (tools/elolib.py fit / expected / field_score: Bradley-Terry MLE with prior, pair cap, Elo scale, SE)

The code computes its documented formula correctly. An independent Newton-Raphson fit (numpy; its own CSV parsing, dedupe and pair cap; elolib.fit is not called) gives the same answer on the real progress/games.csv. The data has 10,976 rows, 10,841 after dedupe, and 102 players. Newton converges in 8 iterations (0.13 s); MM needs 7,375 iterations (2.9 s). Max |rating difference| is 8.7e-6 Elo, a near-uniform level offset from MM's slow mode, largest for andrewkbank.First, Yooncw0223.lec3player and addiesteward.NDeClaw. Max |SE difference| is 7.4e-8 (us:examplefuncsplayer). Games and wins agree for all 102 players.

Line by line, the formula checks out:
- Line 78 is Hunter's MM update for L = Σ(Ww_i+prior)·ln s_i − Σ_pairs n_ij·ln(s_i+s_j) − 2·prior·Σ ln(s_i+1). The parallel (Jacobi) form is a valid MM because the surrogate separates by player.
- The prior is `prior` virtual wins and losses against an anchor with s=1 (1500).
- SCALE = 400/ln 10 is consistent with expected().
- The pair cap gives n_eff = min(n, cap) and scales wins by the same factor.
- 0-win and all-win players stay finite.
- SE is SCALE/sqrt(diagonal of the observed information in ln-strength), exactly as the docstring says.
- Closed-form check: one game a>b gives 1591.7315 / 1408.2685 with SE 214.1294, matching the analytic solution. The pair cap at caps 200, 0 and 50, and prior=2, also match Newton.

The real problem is statistical, not arithmetic: the "weak" prior is not weak in aggregate. It compresses the whole rating scale, by 13% on the real data and about 19% in a realistic simulation. As a result the published "+- 95%" intervals cover the truth only about 42% of the time.

Parameter recovery used 40 players (30 field bots bootstrapped from the real fitted field; 10 'our builds' uniform 1650-2050) and 3,000 games, with 200 replications per scenario. Most replications were fitted by Newton; elolib.fit itself was run on 30 of them and matched Newton to ≤6.6e-5 Elo with no non-convergence warnings.

Design notes:

- MM convergence is adequate as it stands. The Jacobi MM converges linearly; the stopping rule is on the per-iteration change in ln s (tol 1e-10), and the remaining error is about 500 times the last step: 8.7e-6 Elo on the real data (iters.py, Newton as reference). Real data needs 7,375 iterations; the 3,000-iteration cap the docstring mentions would leave 0.05 Elo of error, 1,000 iterations 2.8 Elo and 300 iterations 14.5 Elo. The simulations needed 4,258 (proximity pairing) and 5,671 (uniform) iterations at prior 1, and about 40,000 at prior 0.1. Newton needs 8 to 10.
- Stale or imprecise docstrings (CLAUDE.md rule 8). 'Convergence takes ~24,000 iterations on ~19,000 games' is now 7,375 iterations on 10,841 distinct games. 'The anchor fixes the scale at 1500' is imprecise: the score equations force Σ_i tanh((R_i−1500)/(2·SCALE)) = 0 exactly (1.0e-6 on elolib's output), so the real mean is 1457 and the median 1530. '200 games pin a pair's win rate to about +-3.5 points' is a 1-SE figure, while elo.py's '+-18 points for 30 games' is a 95% figure.
- The absolute level is a convention set by that tanh identity. It moves whenever the player set changes: adding weak field bots raises everyone with no new information about them. Compare ratings only as differences within one fit, never across fits or against the replica's numbers.
- R and SE are defaultdicts (lines 84). Any lookup of an unknown player returns 1500 / inf and inserts it. field_score() over a field containing an unrated bot silently counts that bot at 1500 (field_score(a, [b, ghost]) = 0.685 vs 0.742 for [b] alone), and 1500 is not even the field's centre. All 87 ladder bots are rated today, so there is no current effect; elo.py:105 passes all of ladder_bots(), so the first unrated addition would distort every field score.
- The pair cap works as documented. With pairs of 1,000/100/10 games it matches Newton at caps 200, 0 and 50. It is never active on the real data: the largest pair has 46 games and 0 of 1,605 pairs exceed 200. For a capped pair the SE assumes cap games, which is conservative: the weighted estimator's true variance scales as 1/n, not 1/cap.
- dedupe's determinism assumption holds on the data. 132 keys repeat, adding 135 rows, and every repeat has the same winner and round count, so dropping repeats loses nothing. Seeds '' and 'map' are documented as the same seed but are distinct keys; there are no '' seeds in the data today. COIN rows are dropped; there are none today.
- The only winless or unbeaten player is us:examplefuncsplayer (0 wins in 6 games, R 1228 ± 380 at 95%). Its rating is mostly prior: at prior 0.01 it falls 929 below the field mean. It is a sensible candidate to exclude from the ladder table.
- A full inverse-Hessian SE ((H^-1)_ii, 1.7x the diagonal SE at the median) answers a different question: it includes the weakly identified common level. The centred (C·H^-1·C) or sandwich SE is the frequentist quantity that matches what the tables imply; in the simulations both gave 94.6% coverage once the prior bias was removed.

Design notes:

- Coin flips cannot be filtered in galaxy rows. All 10,643 galaxy rows have reason '', so the COIN filter in dedupe only works on local and old-replica rows, where it drops 0 rows. 5,076 galaxy games (48%) end at round 2000. Replays bound the coin-flip share: 1 winner disagreement in 130 replayed pairs of 2000-round games gives about 1.5% of 2000-round games, roughly 75 games (95% upper bound 8.4%, about 430). Coin flips add noise but no direction. The tiebreak level could be recovered from the replay's final-round team stats if this ever matters.
- PAIR_CAP = 200 is effectively inert. Determinism bounds the distinct games per pair at 103 maps x 2 spawn assignments = 206. On today's data the busiest deduped pair has 46 games (addiesteward.NDeClaw vs Yooncw0223.lec3player), and 0 of 1,605 pairs exceed 200. The mechanism itself is sound: proportional weight cap/n on every game, order-invariant (verified by reversing the rows), win ratio preserved, no selection bias. Its only visible effect is stronger prior shrinkage (synthetic: a 30-10 pair with cap 10 narrows the X-Y gap from 178.5 to 147.4 Elo and widens SE X from 58.6 to 98.4). Either drop it or document that dedupe already does its job.
- Doc nit in the elolib docstring: '200 games pin a pair's win rate to about +- 3.5 points' is one standard error (sqrt(.25/200) = 3.5%). The 95% interval is +-6.9, and elo.py quotes 95% elsewhere ('+-18 points at 95% for 30 games').
- Player identity is the package name. Today one package maps to one submission (ladder-state.json history), but resubmitting a package under the same name would silently merge two builds into one player. Dedupe would then drop the newer build's games on every (pair, map, orientation) the older build already played, because it keeps the first. tools/scrim-record.py's default label 'bot' has the same hazard. A safer key is the package plus the submission id, or a refusal to record a package name already tied to another submission.
- Side imbalance: the trial panel uses order '+', so our builds play almost all their games from the A spawns, and Bradley-Terry has no side term. The measured A-spawn effect, with ratings held fixed, is -7.4 +- 8.6 Elo field vs field (n = 8,493) and -6.8 +- 7.7 over all games. That is not significant, so absolute ratings of our builds could be biased by at most about 10 Elo, and comparisons between our builds by less. Worth re-measuring as data grows.
- Pooling g_iter0 across sources is acceptable. Split by source: local calibration (random numeric seeds) 1815 +- 84, galaxy 1737 +- 60, old replica 1784 +- 311; local minus galaxy is 78 Elo, z = 1.49.
- elo.py mixes raw and deduped counts. opp_games/opp_wins (used by --challenge and --established) and the header's 'ours' count use raw rows; the tables use deduped counts. 11 bots differ by 1 to 4 games. The --challenge set is identical, and --established 8/10/12/15 returns the same set in a different order.
- Self-play rows (teamA == teamB) would enter the fit: +2 games and +1 win to the shown record, with the rating unchanged at the MM fixed point. There are 0 such rows, scrim-record refuses them, and galaxy cannot produce them. A guard in dedupe would make the invariant explicit.
- Mixed line endings: the header and 333 rows end in CRLF (the csv default in elolib.append and replica export), while the 10,643 galaxy rows end in LF. load() reads both correctly. Cosmetic.
- The elolib docstring says the reason column holds a REASONS code, but galaxy rows hold '' (reason_code('') would give 'OTHER'). The docstring should say galaxy rows carry no reason.
- Fit wall time depends on driver load. One fit of the real file took 3 s in one run and 80 s in another (load average 6.8 on the 2 vCPU driver, other agents running). This is not a data-path issue, but tests that call fit on the real file would be slow.

## Fixes applied

- **F1**: tools/elolib.py: replaced the minorise-maximise loop in fit() with a Newton MAP solve. It uses a pure-Python Cholesky (no numpy), halves the step whenever the penalised log-likelihood would fall, and stops when the largest step is below tol. The default iters is now 100. The new PRIOR = 0.1 (virtual wins and as many losses against 1500, per player) replaces 1.0 as the default. The module docstring's 'weak prior' sentence and the stale '~24,000 iterations' note are rewritten: they now give the reason for 0.1, the tanh level identity, and say to compare only within one fit. Check: on the real games.csv at prior 1 with the old dedupe key, Newton matches the old MM to 9.1e-6 Elo, with identical games and wins. The full fit takes 0.19 s, against 3 s on a quiet driver for the old code.
- **F2**: tools/elolib.py fit(): the SE is now SCALE*sqrt((C I^-1 C)_ii), the SE of R_i minus the mean rating of all fitted players. It comes from the Cholesky factor: (I^-1)_ii is the squared norm of column i of L^-1, plus one solve for I^-1 1. The raw (H^-1)_ii is not used. Docstring updated. On real data at prior 1 the ratio of the new SE to the old diagonal SE is median 1.105, max 1.504, which reproduces the verifier. tools/elo.py: the ELO.md sentence now says '+- 95%, relative to the mean rating of all N players', and the elo.png x-label says '95% interval relative to the mean rating'.
- **F3**: fit() returns empty defaultdicts and Counters before the solve when there are no players, so [] and all-COIN input no longer raise.
- **F5**: fit() drops rows with teamA == teamB after dedupe and prints 'elolib.fit: dropped N self-play rows' to stderr. The returned games and wins no longer include them.
- **F6**: fit() raises ValueError when not prior > 0. This is an explicit raise, not an assert, and it also catches nan.
- **D1**: dedupe() keys on the spawn owners: a 'map-rev' row (X, Y) becomes (Y, X, map, 'map'). Keep-first is unchanged and numeric seeds are untouched. Docstring updated. On the current file, distinct games go from 11,243 to 11,158.
- **D2**: load() keeps only rows whose winner is exactly 'A' or 'B' and prints the skipped count to stderr. dedupe() skips the same rows before the key is added to `seen`, with a stderr count, so callers that pass rows directly are covered too. elo.py's raw-row loops get this through load().
- **D3**: The dedupe key normalises the seed with (r.get('seed') or 'map'), so '' and a missing seed both equal 'map'. This is part of the D1 line.
- **D4**: append() writes the header when the file is missing or has 0 bytes, matching galaxy/results.py and replica/api.py.
- **D5**: elolib.BASELINES = {'examplefuncsplayer'}. is_ours() is now startswith('us:') and not a baseline, so build_of() returns None for it. The baseline's 6 games stay in the fit as an ordinary player. Stale g_iterN docstrings in elo.py and elolib.py are updated. field_score.submissions() now reads elolib.STATE, the same file, so tests can patch it.
- **C3**: elo.py --build exits non-zero with 'elo.py: build X has no rated games in progress/games.csv' instead of printing 1500 +- inf and a field score. --as with a build that has no games prints a note to stderr only, so the pool on stdout is unchanged, and adds '(src/X does not exist: a typo?)' when there is no src/X.
