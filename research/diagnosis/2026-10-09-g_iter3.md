## Lens: ECONOMY

ECONOMY (analysis 2026-10-09 08:45 PDT). Data: g_iter3's panel 1809-1818 (100 games: 52 losses, 48 wins; we are side A in every game, so prog 0 is our HQs), cross-checked on g_iter2's panel 1017-1026 and on g_iter3's 33 ranked matches (99 games). Sources: games, census, timeline, hq, deaths, trips, robots and tele_states CSVs. Scratch scripts are in /tmp/claude-1000/-home-terryvanbelle-projects-vibe-2023/4344f381-983b-4736-adb6-8d7ba2ecdeb6/scratchpad/diag/g1-g26.py. Everything here was read-only: no games were run and no repo file was touched. Short answer: after r200 the stall is mostly about where our resources go, not how much we gather. Our carriers do about as well per deposited trip as the opponents'. But in losses roughly half of our adamantium after r200 goes into anchors that are lost (77%) or into banks at besieged HQs. A quarter of our mana goes into those same lost anchors. Carriers then die about 3x as often per trip near our own HQs. On Forest and MassiveL the carriers also queue for hundreds of turns at one mana well. The early adamantium shortfall in r100-300 (c_eco2's target) is real, but it is the smaller part of the r200+ gap.

### Proposed arms

- **c_anch1** (small): Stop investing in anchors when they are likely to be lost, and stop the late adamantium role that exists to fund them. (1) HQ.islandToTake(): an island counts only if Comms.islandOwner(id)==0 AND Comms.islandTile(id)!=null, i.e. a reported neutral island. Unreported islands no longer trigger anchors (today they do, which produces the E-state anchors). (2) Team pressure bit: each HQ keeps an integer moving average of G.nEnemyFighters (ema16 += 16*n - ema16/8). It sets bit 4+hqIdx of Comms slot 61 (AD_FLAGS, bits 4-7 unused today) when the average reaches C.PRESS_ON=3 fighters and clears it below C.PRESS_OFF=1. (3) wantAnchor also requires: no pressure bit set AND enemy-held islands (owner 2) <= ours (owner 1), counted from the island slots. From round C.ANCHOR_GATE_UNTIL=1500 the gate is off (tiebreak horizon). (4) Carrier.swarmRole(): the late adamantium role (round >= AD_ROLE_FROM and id % AD_LATE_EVERY == 0) applies only while the gate is open. Otherwise the early rule applies: adamantium only while the nearest HQ's want-Ad flag is set.
  Mechanism: In losses, 77% of anchors (9.5 of 12.4 per game) are lost with their carrier. Anchors take 24-31% of mana income and 30-45% of adamantium spending in r300-600, and the anchor-reserve exit blocks the HQ build loop in 15-27% of HQ turns. The pressure or island-deficit gate fires on 52-53% of anchors in losses but only 0-7% of anchors in wins, on both panels. The known-island rule removes the E anchors (39% of anchors lost in losses; all of ReverseFunnel's waste, where 0 of 15-34 anchors per losing game were placed). In losses, the late adamantium role only feeds a bank that grows by as much as we mine. Moving those carriers back to mana feeds launchers in the phase where we are being out-launchered 9-10 to 21-29 per 100 rounds.
  Expected: Losses: anchors built down 50-60% (about -6.5 per game), returning about 500 Ad and 500 Mn per loss game. Launchers built in r300-700 up 1.5-2.5 per 100 rounds (from 9-10); carriers up about 1-1.5 per 100 rounds. Anchor-reserve exits in r300-500 from 19-27% of HQ turns to under 10%. Mana collected in r300-600 up 10-15% as adamantium-role carriers return to mana. Wins: placements nearly unchanged (4% of win placements fall in gated buckets); on ReverseFunnel the 14-28 anchors per winning game shrink to the placeable ones. Panel: +2 to 4 games, mostly in close matchups (NotLLeon 5-5, georgezhang 6-4, pranayagra/jmerle 3-7) and on ReverseFunnel and Forest.. Risk: Comebacks by late conquest and tiebreak games are the exposure: 7 of 52 panel losses reached r2000, mitigated by opening the gate from r1500. Islands reported late could delay the first anchor in wins, so check that first_anchor does not move by more than 30 rounds. Self-play rarely presses an HQ, so the local screen will read neutral; this needs the replica panel to judge. The moving average of fighters seen is a proxy for hq.csv pressure34, so calibrate PRESS_ON on one local siege.
- **c_home1** (medium): Make carriers avoid besieged HQs, using c_anch1's pressure bits. (1) HQState.nearestSafe(from): the nearest HQ without a pressure bit, falling back to the nearest HQ. Carrier.deliver, Carrier.survive (the flee-toward-home branch), explore and takeAnchor use it instead of HQState.nearest. (2) Carrier.gather and switchWell skip wells within r2 36 of a fresh enemy sighting (Comms.nearestEnemy(well, 1), i.e. at most about 32 rounds old) and wells within r2 50 of a pressed HQ. Take the nearest remaining well of the role's type; else of any type, with the role following the well for that trip; else wait next to the safe HQ. (3) After a flee turn (state F or V), drop the well (well = null) so the next gather picks again under rule (2) instead of walking back into the same launchers.
  Mechanism: After r200 in losses, 23% of our trips end in death (opponents 6-9%), a median 17 rounds after leaving home. 62% of those turns are spent walking to the well and 88% of those trips include flee turns. 35% of our carrier deaths happen next to a pressed HQ, and 62% of those while fleeing toward it, because survive() flees toward the nearest home. Pressed HQs already get about 0 deliveries (0.7 Ad, 1.0 Mn per 25 rounds), so sending carriers to unthreatened HQs loses nothing there. Those HQs are 'poor' (mean 43 Ad) and convert deliveries straight into carriers and launchers.
  Expected: Share of trips ending in death after r200 in losses: 23% down to 13-16%. Vs the top 3: 20-25% down to about 12%. Live carriers at r500 in losses: 11.8 up to 15-17. Collection per carrier-round in r200-400 of losses: 0.31-0.33 up to 0.36-0.40. Carriers built per 100 rounds in r300-600 up about 1, through the extra deliveries to unthreatened HQs.. Risk: Longer hauls to a distant unthreatened HQ on large maps, possibly through enemy launchers. No effect on 1-HQ maps (BatSignal) or when all HQs are pressed (falls back to today's behaviour). With only 4 sighting slots a threat can go unrecorded, and wells blacklisted too eagerly cost trips, so keep the radius tight. Bytecode cost is small: a few shared-array reads per well pick.
- **c_well2** (small): Add a queue valve so carriers stop queueing for one mana well on maps like Forest and MassiveL. (1) Patience for the going-to-well state: count G and W turns in the current trip. Past max(C.G_PATIENCE=30, 3 x the Chebyshev distance to the well) turns without a collect, call switchWell. (2) switchWell for mana carriers tries, in order: known mana wells with fewer allied carriers in view than the current one; then predicted mana wells on our half, i.e. images of known enemy-half mana wells under the decided symmetry (MapMem.img, as Launcher.computePredWells already builds), rejected after a sighting shows none; then, from r150 and only while the delivery HQ holds under 150 Ad, the nearest adamantium well, with the role switched for that trip only. (3) Suspend the late adamantium role for carriers whose delivery HQ already holds 150 Ad or more.
  Mechanism: On Forest our carriers spend 57% of trip turns walking to a well (64% for g_iter2), against 6-16% on most other maps. 27-77 of our deposits per game go through mana well (8,12) at 100-215-round cycles with 34-135 walking turns per trip, while the opponent uses 5-7 wells including the (29,48)/(30,48) pair. Forest collection per carrier-round: 0.19 vs 0.27; MassiveL: 0.28 vs 0.41 with 45% of turns walking. On Forest, carriers alive are close (42 vs 54 at r300) but we collect half the mana (1,767 vs 3,333 in r100-500) while banking 682 Ad. The carrier count is not the problem there: the carriers are idle in a queue, and the adamantium carriers feed an unused bank.
  Expected: Forest and MassiveL collection per carrier-round up 30-40% (0.19 to about 0.26; 0.28 to about 0.37). Forest mana r100-500 up 600-900. Forest launchers built in r100-500: 62 up to 75-80 (opponent 98). Panel: +1 to 3 games on Forest and MassiveL, each at 2/10 today. Little effect elsewhere, where walking turns are 6-16% and the patience rarely runs out.. Risk: A carrier leaving a queue for a far or wrongly predicted well wastes a trip; predictions are rejected on sight. Switching to adamantium wells once inflated adamantium at r100 (v1 Ad@100 164 vs members' 10-92); the r150 floor and the 150-Ad bank test limit that. A switch can push a carrier into contested ground; combined with c_home1's sighting filter that risk is smaller.

### Proposed arms

- **c_def1** (medium): HQ raid alarm and recall. (1) Each HQ writes min(7, G.nEnemyFighters) every turn, only when the value changes, into 3 free bits of Comms slot AD_FLAGS (bits 4+3*hqIdx; bits 0-3 stay the Ad flags, and setAdFlag already preserves other bits). (2) Launcher.pickObjective: after the local-sighting check (within r2 64) and before islands/raid/ring, take the HQ_i with alarm >= C.RAID_ALARM (2). If the launcher is within r2 C.RECALL_R2 (400, about 20 tiles or 40 rounds) of HQ_i and closer to HQ_i than to its siege target (siegeTarget(MapMem.enemyHQs())), set objective = HQ_i's tile (rally on the HQ, not on the sighting), objKind = 0 and objEff = 7. (3) Launcher.regroup: a recalled launcher skips the forming hold, i.e. the existing HOME_DEFENCE early return also fires on a recall. Lone launchers in the field still regroup as now. (4) Optional: count the HQ as one ally in fight()'s 'ahead' test when within r2 13 of our own HQ.
  Mechanism: Before r300 the combat deficit is at home (-1352 per game summed over the five strong teams), and it grew by 502 when navigation sped our army to the front. Raids are 4-5 enemy launchers against a peak of 1 defender, with only 7-11% of our army present, and raids where we match their count are won 57-73%. Today launchers react only to sightings within r2 64. An HQ alarm with a count calls back the launchers already on our half, so they arrive within the median raid duration (11-23 rounds).
