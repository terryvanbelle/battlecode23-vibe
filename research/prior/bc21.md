# Prior project: battlecode21-vibe (Battlecode 2021, Enlightenment Centers / politicians / slanderers / muckrakers) -- research report

Slice `bc21`, 4th attempt. Written 2026-10-07 for the Battlecode 2023 ("Tempest") practice project.
Sources: the filtered reading-room copies under `reference/readroom-no2023/bc21/` for every
`.md`/`.txt`, and code read directly from `reference/battlecode21-vibe/`. Numbers are quoted as the
documents wrote them. Anything marked **[inference]** is mine, not the 2021 project's. Anything marked
**[verify in 2023]** is a 2021 engine fact that probably carries over but has not been checked against
engine 3.0.15.

The project in one paragraph: one human owner directing Claude Code, 16-23 September 2026, 448
commits, 63 numbered iterations, 13 accepted snapshots (`src/g_iter1`..`g_iter13`). Games were
expensive (15 s to 45 min, ~5.7 CPU-minutes each), and the whole method was shaped by that cost. Final
submission `g_iter13`: 334/432 (77.3%) over nine 48-game scrimmage blocks on a fixed field of eight
released 2021 bots, 33% against the top bot (awesomelemonade), 46% against the second (rzhan11), Elo
1742, rank 3 of 21 rated bots, from a first contest-rules block of 14/48 (29%).

## 2. What worked (evidence as written)

The project measured with three instruments in turn (section 4.2): a paired 24/72-cell head-to-head
and roster (Iterations 1-21), a 48-cell panel (Iterations 21-22), and an SPRT mirror on random maps
(Iteration 22 onward), with 48-game scrimmage blocks as the contest signal from 2026-09-17. "Ladder"
below means those blocks.

### 2.4 Method and infrastructure that worked

- **SPRT mirror on random maps** replaced a panel that "could not have accepted anything we were able
  to build": the very next candidate was accepted at 68.8%.
- **Diagnostic game before any test** (owner rule, PROMPTS 31): caught three inert candidates
  (opening capture fired 0 times; garrison guards walked 4-9 tiles off post; collapse screen had 397
  holds and 0 moves) and later two "obviously missing" mechanisms rejected in minutes (nearest-first
  targets, chip captures).
- **Block mining** (`scrim-study.sh`, `onset.py`, `log-scan.sh`, `econ-scan.sh`): the source of
  Iterations 27, 34, 35, 48, 58.
- **Sparring partners that reproduce a loss condition and are competitive**: `arch_hunt` (5/8 vs
  `g_iter13`) and `arch_lemon` (6/8) were "the first partners that beat the current build".
- **Fixed scrimmage field** (from PROMPTS 74): the first like-for-like ladder comparisons (Iteration
  43's 31/48 vs 35/48 was a clean reject on the ladder).
- **Unit tests for pure bot logic and the analysis pipeline**, including invariants between tuning
  constants; caught a CSV column misalignment that had been "reporting cumulative moves as map
  coverage".
- **Fail-closed guards in tooling** (`tier-check.sh`, `scrim.sh` refusing a missing `games.csv`,
  `gauntlet.sh` refusing to recompile a class tree in use) -- each added after a silent failure.

## 3. What did not work / closed directions

Grouped by theme. Rates are SPRT mirror results unless stated.

**Reallocating influence between unit types (the most consistent negative).**
- Minimum slanderer size 63 (Iter 8): income +30-50% by r200, but head-to-head 12/23 and 0/8 at Stage
  0 -- "the income fed the same sinks".
- Big standing guards (Iter 19): mirror 4/12 (all vote losses), roster 2 v 5 at 13 cells.
- Invest the surplus in big slanderers (Iter 20, re-tested as 29): roster 12 v 14; SPRT 75-85 = 46.9%.
- Muckrakers with real conviction (Iter 28): exposures 0 -> 342 and buff 0 -> 8,881 in the diagnostic,
  enemy slanderers 95 -> 16, and still 33-47 (41.2%) at 150 influence and 19-45 (29.7%) at 50. "In
  self-play the compounding side wins even when the hunting works spectacularly."
- Army: 300+ attack politicians from the spare branch (Iter 13): 9 hostile ECs converted in one game,
  roster 31 vs 33, head-to-head 6/14 (12 of 14 decided on votes).
- `METHOD.md` summary: "Five reallocations (guards, hunters, deposits, surplus, capture reserve) were
  all rejected; all three accepts removed a constraint or a bug."

**Banking / reserves / floors.**
- Capture bank 300 (Iter 11, 3 arms): captured ECs held 5x longer, 31 vs 33 on 68 cells.
- EC influence floor (Iter 17, hard and sink-only): hard floor starved slanderers (0 from r450);
  sink-only level in the mirror (13/24) and roster 6 v 9 with rush losses at r519-521.
- Reserve for the next neutral (Iter 32): 33-47 = 41.2% -- decisive on a map with four visible cheap
  neutrals, "pure cost elsewhere".
- Release the economy under siege (Iter 39 / 43): mirror 32-48 (40.0%); ladder 31/48 vs 35/48. Spend the
  siege bank on defenders (Iter 45): 34/48 vs 35/48. Conclusion: "A besieged centre is right to hoard."

**Expansion / capture mechanics.**
- Chip-politician neutral race (Iter 5, 3 arms): 0/8, 1/8, 0/8; economy starved.
- Opening capture bank (Iter 16, 3 doses): 0/13, 0/4, 2/8. It took neutrals (Arena: ECs 5 v 2 at r450)
  and lost them at 5-61 influence each.
- Capture-and-hold garrison (Iter 21, 3 doses): +3, -4 (hugging guards sealed the spawn ring), +1 on the
  48-cell panel.
- Capturer cap 2 -> 4 alone (Iter 31): "exactly 120-120 over 240 games". Accepted later only with its
  enablers (Iter 37).
- Captured EC broadcasts its tile, alone (Iter 36): 65-79 = 45.1% despite fixing a real bug (41 of 54
  capture builds walked to ground already held). It became half of the accepted Iter 37.
- Claims lapse / cap 8 (Iter 41): "the blocking is conserved: raise the cap and claims block, lapse the
  claims and the cap blocks". Nearest-first targets (Iter 42): flips 33 -> 14. Chip captures (Iter 44):
  flips 14 -> 2, final ECs 2 v 6.
- Hold what we take -- 4 diagonal sentinels, floor, capture bank 110 (Iter 52): ladder 38/48 vs 40/48;
  SPRT 15-33 = 31.2%. Its fixed-seed result (5 ECs to 3) "was a property of that seed".
- Target-aware attack (53, 61), rich guards (54, 57, 59), waves (55, inert): null or negative.

**Defence near the EC.**
- EC wall of 1-influence muckrakers (Iter 3): per-hit dilution real (0.2-0.35x) but 9/72 vs 13/72 on
  targets; the wall's build slots cost more than it absorbed.
- Threat-sized guards, else bank (Iter 10): decision point unreachable -- the EC sees a 600+ attacker
  3-5 rounds before it speaks.
- Guards collapse onto EC tiles under threat (Iter 23): stack 70-74 (48.6%), about -5 points.
- Guards among the slanderers with a threat-only bar (Iter 60): beat both sparring partners on their
  seeds, then SPRT 2-14 in batch 1 and ladder 19-13 at 32 of 48.

**Scouting / coverage (a marker, not a lever).**
- Eight early scouts (Iter 14): all neutrals known by r200, roster 18 vs 23 at 40 cells.
- Scouts keep sweeping (Iter 33): coverage 50.6% -> 65.9%, 4 ECs at r400 vs 2 in the diagnostic, SPRT
  124-116 = 51.7%. Re-tested as Iter 63 (scout caps doubled): ladder 35/48 with strong bots 2/12.
- The 144-game final onset table still ranks coverage lead (+0.49) and muckraker-count lead (+0.47)
  first at r200; the log's verdict is that this is "a *marker* of games we win, not a lever we can pull
  with scouts alone".

**Slanderer safety and bidding.**
- Relay flee (Iter 12, re-tested as 24): exposures halved in the diagnostic, but SPRT 34-46 = 42.5%:
  "a slanderer that spends its turn fleeing a relayed position is not earning".
- Yield the bid war (Iter 26): 68-76 = 47.2%. Saving mode dose 2, repeated saves (Iter 25): 67-77 = 46.5%.
- Cheap guards unconditionally (Iter 56): ladder 37/48 with awesomelemonade 3/6, but SPRT 30-34
  (stopped) -- the mirror's 300-1,000 attackers convert 15-influence guards wholesale. Made
  conditional, it became `g_iter13`.

**Navigation.** A local Dijkstra over a 5x5 window was written and **reverted unrun** after
`--navstats` showed our units already moved slightly more per unit than the bot beating us (86.1 vs
84.0) and stepped on low-passability tiles less (16.2% vs 35.8%); the real gap was coverage (46.4% vs
92.8%).

**Copying the opponent's allocation.** `arch_big`, rzhan11's measured allocation rebuilt faithfully
(51% economy, 30% muckrakers, 19% politicians), lost 0-24 to our own bot: "the allocation was
downstream of their advantage, not its source."

**Open at the end.** How to answer a muckrakers swarm without buying ~100 guards by r250; where our
slanderers should live (ours were hunted -- 12 of 16 and 47 died in r250-500 against 2 and 6 of
theirs); what to do with a 20-40k bank in losses; how to cash cheap early votes.

## 4. Method lessons

### 4.1 The training loop (TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md, written fresh from 2022/2025/2026 predecessors + bcenv)

Principles: instruments before strategy; "the engine is the truth" (tag every fact with where it was
verified); trace then theorise; kill ideas as cheaply as possible (engine read < replay census < 1-map
probe < screen < full evaluation); pre-register; record measurements not explanations; absolute
strength is the objective; never idle.

Phase 0 checklist, all of which ran: rules digest cross-checked against engine source; bare-`java`
headless runner (no Gradle per game); determinism check (identical code twice must be byte-identical;
2021's only randomness was the final `Math.random` coin flip, which a bot that bids once never
reaches); replay reader; bytecode monitor in the bot from day one; mirror harness; play-symmetry audit
("any fixed-order iteration over directions, any first result from a sensing call whose scan order is
fixed ... gives one side a compounding tempo edge"); progress charts. Six "basics" each get a
diagnostic metric (economy, navigation, exploration, symmetry inference, combat micro, bytecode
safety).

Per candidate (4.1-4.7): select a target (a loss against a target/peer-tier opponent, an absolute
degeneracy in our own replays, or a capability gap); trace it; pre-register mechanism, decision-point
counter, reachability, trigger frequency, price, history, gate, falsifier, dose ladder; one change per
candidate (relaxed by the owner: combined candidates allowed when pre-registered with counters per
half); **diagnostic game first**; gate; post-accept routine (snapshot, archetype regression, ladder
block, re-tier, block study, ledger, commit and push). Functional-area map plus `MaxRejectsPerArea` 3
and `SwingEvery` 4 (a structural attempt at least every fourth). Closed-directions ledger with a
re-open condition per row -- grep it before opening anything.

What actually produced accepts, in the project's own words (`POSTMORTEM` section 9): "Build the
measurement before the bot ... Log what the bot *hears* and *decides*, at the decision point, with a
counter you can grep. Every accept this season came from a counter that said 'this never fires' or
'this fires 520 times'."

### 4.2 Gating and statistics -- the instrument history, in order

1. **24-game quick-set head-to-head + 72-cell paired roster (McNemar)**, Iterations 1-20. Noise floor
   measured with an inert pair (RNG seed offset 12345 -> 12346): 12/24; binomial sd ~2.4 games, so
   `AcceptMargin` +5 on 24. Paired-cell analysis: "with D flips the net margin must reach ~2*sqrt(D)
   (D=8: +6, D=12: +8, D=24: +10)"; detectable effect at 80% power ~4 cells on 24, ~7 on 72, ~13-15 on
   216. Futility stop: after 36 of 72 shared cells, stop when 3+ behind (a reject then costs ~40 games
   instead of ~100). Discovered blind spot: three candidates in a row early-stopped at exactly 6/14
   (relay, army, scouts) -- the twin did not punish what they fixed.
2. **Contest rules** (PROMPTS 25, 2026-09-17): chosen-map play against external bots forbidden; the
   roster check and tiering scans became illegal.
3. **48-cell panel** (24 mirror cells + 24 archetype cells, +5 net). "Eight candidates in a row were
   rejected ... 24 mirror cells resolve only an edge above roughly 75%; a real 60% improvement needs
   about 190 games, and the archetype half is capped at +4 because the incumbent already wins 20 of its
   24."
4. **SPRT mirror** (2026-09-18): `tools/mirror.sh` + `tools/sprt.py`, candidate vs incumbent, random
   map and side per game, batches of 16, H0 p=0.50 vs H1 p=0.58 ("about 56 Elo"), alpha = beta = 0.05,
   LLR bounds +/-2.94, cap 240. Accepts came in 32-208 games. Separating 55% from 50% "needs roughly
   800 games (~11 VM-hours)".
5. **Stacking policy** for the common inconclusive case: inconclusive and >= 53% over >= 200 games ->
   keep provisionally in `src/bot`, no snapshot; further candidates stack on top and are gated against
   the incumbent; the stack snapshots when it reaches ACCEPT. Worked twice (38 rode 40; 47 rode 48).
6. **METHOD 5b: when the mirror is the wrong opponent, add a pre-registered arm** (triggered by PROMPTS
   71). A defensive change against something the incumbent never does reads ~50% in the mirror. Rule:
   name the archetype that has the property *before* the gate; accept a mirror-neutral change only if
   it clearly wins the arm. "If you find yourself reaching for the argument after a disappointing gate,
   you have already failed the test." `g_iter13` was accepted this way.
7. **Archetypes need two verified properties** (from `arch_siege`): (1) reproduce the condition the
   change targets (measure the condition), (2) win 25-75% against the incumbent, or nothing can vary
   -- `arch_siege` blocked the EC 1,260 of 1,500 rounds and both builds swept it 21/21.
8. **Fixed-seed single games are a filter, never a verdict.** Iterations 52, 60, 62, 63 each won
   fixed-seed games and failed both gate and ladder.
9. **Never read a running batch.** Games ending by capture finish first, so an early tally is the
   losses: a 0-5 mid-batch read that ended 11-5 voided a batch (`mirror.sh` gained `W0`/`L0` resume).

**The central measured tension**: self-play gains do not reliably transfer. Accepts worth +19 and +9
points in the mirror moved the ladder by nothing; `g_iter5` "won its mirror 68.8% and scored 12/48
against 14/48". Rule adopted: "Distrust the proxy when a *series* of self-play accepts never moves the
rating; never on a single block." Owner rule (PROMPTS 37): self-play is the *only* gate, because a real
team cannot scrimmage without submitting; a submission whose block's Wilson upper bound falls below the
previous submission's point estimate is withdrawn.

### 4.3 Correlation and onset as a hypothesis generator (owner-driven, PROMPTS 43-56)

- `scrim-study.sh` dumps both sides' metrics every 50 rounds for **wins and losses** (`KEEP_ALL=1`);
  `correlate.py` gives point-biserial correlation per metric plus a **within-opponent-and-map**
  correlation; `onset.py` finds the first round each correlation crosses +0.30 and holds.
  `polarity.py` orients every metric so positive is always good for us.
- Traps, each paid for: **outcome leakage** (at r600 nearly everything reads +0.53 to +0.61; act on
  r200); **opponent strength** (fixed by the within column); **symptom vs cause** (no statistical fix:
  `arch_big` 0-24; coverage 51.7%); **map-size confounding** (raw coverage counts compared across 32x32
  and 64x64 maps produced a false "scouting is wildly inconsistent" finding; only us-minus-them gaps
  survive); **sign errors** (gaps of inverted metrics were unoriented until a unit test pinned them).
- Noise floor ~2/sqrt(n): 0.29 for 48 games, 0.19 for 114-144.
- Division of labour the project claimed and demonstrated: "The correlation ranking pointed at
  'expansion'; the diagnostic found the mechanism." Owner, PROMPTS 73: "we took a weakness and turned
  it into an advantage."

### 4.4 Ladder / Elo design

- External bots: 96 GitHub repos found, 459 bot packages compiled from 66 repos without reading source
  (`bench-compile.sh`), one bot per repo chosen by directory name only (65 on the ladder list).
- Played **only as scrimmages**: random map from the released corpus (Cow excluded), random side,
  rotating opponents (never the same twice in a row, each at most ceil(N/pool) per block). Our own
  snapshots and archetypes stay unrestricted.
- **Elo from our games only** (PROMPTS 27: external-vs-external is a waste of VM): K=32 from 1500; our
  builds share one player `us` (a new version inherits the rating); unmet bots unrated. Per-build
  records with Wilson 95% intervals (`elo.py --build`).
- **Pool selection evolved three times**: (a) the 20-50% band from tiering scans (pre-contest-rules);
  (b) `--pool 6 --explore 2` (six rated bots just above us + two least-met) -- which silently never ran

  field from 9 to 21 bots and made consecutive blocks incomparable, `--established 8`: a **fixed field
  of the eight most-played bots**. Lesson: with a rotating pool, "block-to-block comparison is only
  possible on the intersection, and the intersection changes every time"; a fixed field makes win

- **Elo inflation by new weak opponents**: `g_iter9`'s 1511 -> 1692 came largely from four newcomers

- The final 77.3% is flattered by the field: six of the eight fixed opponents are beaten ~90%; the

  are the project's]**. Over the last day's blocks (mostly `g_iter13`, plus three rejected-candidate

- **Tier rule** (owner charter): no replay of a game against a bot we beat < 20% ("locked"); 20-50%

## 5. Bot architecture and the basics (with 2023 mapping)

### 5.1 Structure

`RobotPlayer.run` builds a per-type controller (`EC`, `Politician`, `Slanderer`, `Muckraker`), all
extending `Robot`. `Robot.loop()` is the turn loop with the bytecode monitor; `sense()` buckets
`senseNearbyRobots()` once into fixed arrays (`enemies[64]`, `friends[64]`), tracks nearest enemy and
nearest enemy muckraker, and feeds `MapState` sightings. Static fields are per-robot memory. All
tunables live in `C.java`, each with a comment citing the iteration and measurement that set it ("so a
dose ladder is a one-line diff"); `C.ARCHETYPE` turns the same code into sparring partners via
`tools/snapshot.sh <name> <int>`. `Debug.log` prints `@tag k=v` lines gated by `C.DEBUG`; logging cost
measured as zero wall time (416 s vs 416 s with DEBUG off).

**2023 mapping:** steal the skeleton as-is (Java 8 both years). A slanderer-camouflage hand-off
(`Slanderer` keeps running and wraps a `Politician` controller when `rc.getType()` changes) has no
2023 analogue, but the pattern "check type every turn, the engine does not restart your code" is worth
a test **[verify in 2023]** for anything that changes in place (e.g. a carrier picking up an anchor).

### 5.2 Bytecode

`Robot.loop()` records `Clock.getBytecodeNum()` after each turn; if `rc.getRoundNum()` changed during
the turn the robot overran (`bcOver++`); near-misses are turns above 90% of the limit; `@bc
t=<type> used= max= near= over=` every 50 turns. `EC.profLog` logs per-stage bytecodes (`@bcprof`) when
a turn passes 15k. `ReplayDump --bytecode` cross-checks with the engine's own per-robot bytecode field.

Key fact: "A robot over its bytecode budget loses whole rounds, silently." Iteration 49's extra flag
reads pushed the home EC to 20k; it lost 213-404 rounds a game vs 0-19 for the incumbent, and fixes
worth ~+10% gated at 53%. Cuts that worked: scan only live capturers instead of all 96 children; compact
child arrays only after a death; absorb each flag value once per turn (`seenThisTurn`); move sibling
and neighbour reads to the off-turn (EC builds every other round). Also: `java.util` is counted as your
code; `Object.getClass()`/`getStackTrace()` in `Debug` caused "Team is known to have errors" and killed
every robot at spawn.

Game speed (measured, 2021): single Gridlock game 252.5 s; six parallel games 3.7x not 6x (68.1 s
amortised on 64x64, **31.2 s on 32x32**, i.e. 2.2x cheaper); ~53 games/hour on an 8-vCPU box; a 200-game
SPRT ~3.8 h. GC/JIT flags, indicators and replay writing were all within noise. Heavy bots exhausted the
JVM code cache (`compilation: disabled`) -> `-XX:ReservedCodeCacheSize=512m`.

**2023 mapping:** port the monitor and `@bcprof` unchanged; limits become HQ 20,000, carrier 12,500,
others 10,000 **[verify in 2023 that an overrun still silently loses the rest of the turn]**. Shared-array
reads/writes and multi-build HQ turns are the likely hot spots **[inference]**. Re-measure game cost on
day one with `bench-flags.sh`/`bench-throughput.sh`; 2023 has 2,000 rounds and 20x20..60x60 maps, so
the per-game cost and the small-map discount must be measured, not assumed.

### 5.3 Economy and the production chain

`EC.build()` is a single ordered if/else chain -- "the order is the strategy". Final order: opening
slanderer at r1 (if `OPENING_SLANDERER_FIRST`), early scouts, emergency guard under `danger`, (save mode,
off), affordable neutral capture (`captureAffordable`: cheapest known neutral at `inf + 14`, not claimed
by a live capturer, not presumed), attack on the enemy EC when rich, slanderers to `MAX_SLANDERERS` 20
at `Econ.bestSize` breakpoints (21, 41, 63, 85, 107, 130, ...; buying between two wastes the difference),
standing guards (15 under a swarm, else 20-60), scouts to a growing cap, more slanderers, rich guards,
"rich and idle" all-in capture, then the spare branch (economy to 40 first, then guards to 12, then
1-influence bodies). `econDanger` gates only slanderers; `noTile`/`idle` counters expose a silently
blocked or idle EC. Bidding: shrink 10% after a won vote, grow 25%+1 after a lost one, cap
influence/30 before r600, /5 to r1000, /3 after; stop when mathematically safe; `enemyVotesEst`
initialised at birth to `round - 1 - teamVotes`.

Strategic findings that generalise (all measured in 2021):
- **The opening deployment is worth more than any later reallocation** (+22 points, Iteration 34).
- **Find the constraint that binds before tuning** -- three accepts were cap/defect removals.
- **Never let a rule stop the economy for something that cannot hurt it** (Iteration 27: one
  1-influence enemy scout within six tiles stopped all slanderer production; strong opponents build
  119-678 of them).
- **A surplus that becomes standing bodies is a surplus wasted** (Iteration 35), yet **a besieged
  centre is right to hoard** (Iterations 43/45): spending under pressure lost 1-4 games per 48.
- **Repairs of your own defects transfer better than reallocations.**
- **A unit population that only grows will eventually wall in its producer** (Iteration 40).

**2023 mapping [inference]:**
- Opening: the 2021 lesson is "put the whole starting budget into income at round 1, scout second".
  The 2023 analogue to test first is the HQ's first turns: how many carriers vs launchers, and to
  which wells. HQ cooldown 2 vs decrement 10 means several builds per turn, so the scarce resource is
  Ad/Mn, not build slots (2021's scarce resource was build slots: ~750 builds per EC per game).
- Breakpoints: carrier move cooldown `floor(5 + 3m/8)` grows with load (5 empty, 20 at 40 kg); the
  load at which a carrier turns home is the analogue of slanderer sizing. Compute the
  throughput-optimal load per well distance offline, as `Econ.BREAK` was, and unit-test it.
- `econDanger`: do not stop carrier production or carrier mining because one enemy carrier or
  amplifier is in vision; stop only for a launcher in range.
- Spawn jam: carriers queueing to deposit at an HQ, or launchers idling around it, are the 2023 form of
  Iteration 40's 63 guards walling in a centre. Count "HQ wanted to build and had no legal tile" from
  day one.
- Standing guards: the 2023 HQ deals 4 damage/round within r^2 9 for free, so the case for standing
  launcher guards is weaker than 2021's guard case **[inference]**.
- Tiebreaks: 2021's "a young centre that stops bidding loses the vote race" and "early votes are nearly
  free against some opponents" map onto the 2023 r2000 tiebreak chain (islands held, anchors placed,
  elixir, mana, adamantium): cheap tiebreak points are real and must not be silently dropped by a
  counter that resets **[inference]**.

### 5.4 Navigation (`Nav.java`)

Greedy step minimising `cheb(n, target) + 1/passability(n)` plus a tiny Euclidean tie-break; an
oscillation guard forbids the last 6 positions; after 2 blocked turns or 4 turns without distance
progress it switches to bug wall-following with a per-robot handedness (from the id-seeded RNG) and
leaves bug mode once closer than ever before; `fleeFrom` for slanderers. Measured A-B-A oscillation
0.1-3.4% of moves vs the example bot's 5.1-5.5%. The Dijkstra upgrade was reverted unrun because the
symptom it addressed did not exist (section 3).

**2023 mapping:** the greedy-plus-bug core and the oscillation guard port directly. 2023 terrain is
walls, clouds (20% cooldown penalty, vision r^2 4) and currents (push at end of turn); score
"tiles whose current pushes you backwards" and clouds into the step cost, and measure with a
`--navstats` equivalent (moves per unit, A-B-A rate, coverage) before writing a planner **[inference]**.
Launchers have move cooldown 20 (one move every two turns at decrement 10), so a wasted step is
expensive **[inference from the task's numbers]**.

### 5.5 Exploration

Muckraker scouts: before map bounds are known, head for an edge nobody has found (split by id, with a
sideways component); probe edges with `rc.onTheMap` at the sensing radius (~5 bytecodes per probe);
once bounds are known, visit candidate enemy-EC positions under surviving symmetry hypotheses (split by
id), else random far points; re-pick a waypoint after 12 stuck rounds; from Iteration 48 only one scout
in four camps the enemy EC (`CAMP_ONE_IN`), the rest keep sweeping, and scouts first call on own ECs
they have not visited (courier rule).

Measured: coverage lead is the earliest strong correlate of winning (+0.49 at r200 within pair), but
manufacturing coverage did not win (51.7%, then 35/48). By r200 nobody covers much of a large map
(our coverage 22.4% of a 45x32 map, 2.2% of a 64x64).

**2023 mapping:** bounds are given by the API in 2023 **[verify in 2023: `rc.getMapWidth/Height`]**, so
the edge-probing code becomes unnecessary. Keep coverage and "first sighting of each well/island/enemy
HQ" as standing metrics, but treat them as markers; the 2021 evidence is that scouting more is not by
itself a lever.

### 5.6 Symmetry (`MapState.java`)

Three hypotheses as bits {rotation, mirror-x, mirror-y}; `image(l, h)` maps a tile under each;
`pruneWithEnemyEC` keeps hypotheses under which a sighted enemy EC is the image of one of ours;
`pruneWithEmptyTile` drops a hypothesis when the predicted image of home holds no EC; resets to all
three on contradiction (multi-EC maps). Unit-tested (`MapStateTest`: images self-inverse, bounds,
registry).

**2023 mapping:** 2023 maps are symmetric by rotation or reflection, so the same three hypotheses and
`image()` port directly with known bounds (`W-1-x`, `H-1-y`). Extra contradiction evidence in 2023:
walls, wells (and their type), islands, clouds and currents sensed at a tile vs its image
**[inference]**. 1-4 HQs per team means the "multi-EC map" reset case matters; the HQ-image test should
check against the full own-HQ list, as `pruneWithEnemyEC` does.

### 5.7 Communication (`Comms.java`, EC flag hub)

24-bit flag `[type:4][extra:6][x&127:7][y&127:7]`, locations decoded relative to the reader (coordinates
were offset randomly in 2021). Types: IDLE, ENEMY_EC (sighting with influence bucket), NEUTRAL_EC,
MAP_EDGE, ENEMY_UNIT, ORDER, STATUS, OWN_EC (stamped), OWN_EC_ID, FLIP_INTENT, ENEMY_EC_ECHO (relayed,
stamped). ECs read every child's flag by id (24 per turn round-robin, capture-role children every turn)
and rebroadcast; scouts alternate a fresh sighting with a rotation of durable facts (`cycleFacts`).

The comms findings are the most transferable part of the bot:
1. **Every ownership claim carries a timestamp of the sighting behind it, and the newer claim wins
   whoever relays it** (`MapState.claimEnemy/claimOwn`, stamp = round/32). Without stamps a centre that
   changed hands "ping-ponged between stale echoes" (520 politicians sent at a centre already ours);
   refusing echoes instead threw away a sibling's real sightings (home knew no enemy centre for 1,200
   rounds).
2. **A newborn takes its first turn next round** (the engine iterates a snapshot of execution order):
   a one-round order is gone before it can be read. Every v1 capturer silently ran as a guard.
3. **Robots act in id/spawn order**, so a reader sees what a lower-id unit wrote this round and what a
   higher-id unit wrote last round. FLIP_INTENT exploited this (home hears the capturer's intent the
   turn before the speech that kills it).
4. **A channel can be starved by its own housekeeping**: the two-round spawn ORDER after every build
   meant "a centre that builds most rounds broadcast its map to nobody". Found only by counting what
   each centre heard (`heardNeutral`, `heardOwn`, `orderRounds`, ...).
5. **A list that refuses entries must refuse them for a reason that cannot go stale**: "a tile we own
   is never neutral again" is safe (neutrality only exists at game start); the same rule for enemy
   tiles is not.

**2023 mapping [inference]:** 2023 replaces flags with a 64 x 16-bit shared array, readable anywhere
but writable only within r^2 9 of own HQ, r^2 20 of own amplifier, r^2 4 of own anchored island.
- Stamps (1) map directly: store a coarse round stamp with every well/island/enemy-HQ/threat entry and
  accept only newer claims. Budget bits for it in the array layout from the start.
- The "captured centres are born deaf" problem becomes "units far from an HQ/amplifier cannot write":
  carriers returning to deposit are natural couriers, exactly like Iteration 48's scouts; amplifiers
  are relays. Instrument what each writer actually manages to write and what each reader reads.
- Timing (2, 3): verify in 2023 whether a unit built this round acts this round, and the turn order
  of HQs vs units, before relying on same-round handoffs **[verify in 2023]**.
- Starvation (4): slot contention in a 64-slot array is the analogue; give each message class its own
  slots and count overwrites.

### 5.8 Combat and micro

Politician `bestSpeech(minValue)` evaluates six radii, caches distances once (brought peak bytecode
from 14.2k of 15k down), splits conviction equally over every unit in range (friendly units included),
applies the buff, and values kills by type (muckraker 25 -- `MUCK_IDLE_VALUE` would lower it for a
non-threatening one, but it is 25 in the final build -- slanderer 40, other units 15, each plus half the
target's influence; an EC is worth its influence + 60 if the share flips it), penalising friendly hits. Capturers plan the radius that
maximises the hostile share and step back if friendlies would soak more than 40% of the speech. Measured:
our kills per speech matched or beat rzhan11's in all six games (1.15-1.73 vs 0.91-1.27) while they
spoke up to 3.5x as often -- "we are winning the exchange rate and losing the war by volume" -- which
led to the flat speech bar (Iteration 47, kept at 55.8%).

Engine facts that shaped micro: a speech is split equally over every unit in radius including friends,
so bodies dilute; a converted centre's influence is the overshoot; conversion restarts player code (all
counters zero); the expose buff (`1 + 0.001 x` exposed influence over 50 rounds) reached 6.3x and drained
a 3,500-influence centre in 19 speeches.

**2023 mapping [inference]:** the micro specifics do not carry (launchers deal fixed 20 damage at r^2
16 with a cooldown; no splitting). What carries: measure volume as well as efficiency (attacks
attempted per launcher-turn, damage dealt vs taken) from replays before tuning a threshold; fire
thresholds that scale with a unit's own size made 2021 politicians "fussier as they grew". Kite-and-strike
is the obvious 2023 micro (attack r^2 16 < vision r^2 20); build a scripted brawler sparring partner and
a kills-per-unit-lost metric, as TRAINING_ALGORITHM's basics table asks.

### 5.9 Code quality notes

Readable but dense (one-line multi-statement branches; `EC.java` 33 KB with dozens of diagnostic
counters left in the `@econ` line). Unit tests are plain `main`s (no JUnit) run by `unit-tests.sh`.
`ConstantsTest` asserts relationships between constants (e.g. `SPEND_SLANDERER_CAP >= MAX_SLANDERERS`,
`SPEND_GUARD_CAP < SPEND_SLANDERER_CAP`, `MIN_SLANDERER_SIZE` must be a breakpoint and <= 150) and was
verified to fail when an invariant was broken on purpose. Determinism: no `Math.random`; per-robot LCG
seeded from `rc.getID()`.

## 7. Pitfalls and gotchas that cost time (cost when stated)

1. **Games on the 2 GB driver**: a heavy game is a 1.1-1.4 GB process; two swapped the box (50-minute
   games, 40% I/O wait). A scan ran at 6.7 min/game at 2 jobs until moved to the VM (15 s for the same
   smoke game).
2. **Instrument too weak to accept anything**: eight consecutive rejects under the 48-cell panel; ~28
   VM-hours for five rejected candidates in one afternoon under the 72-cell roster.
3. **Silent bytecode overrun**: candidate home EC lost 213-404 rounds a game; fixes worth ~+10% gated at
   53% until profiled.
4. **Wait predicate that matched nothing** (`pgrep -f gauntlet.sh` while `gauntlet.sh` had re-execed as
   `.reexec-gauntlet.<pid>`): a queued SPRT recompiled `build/classes` under a running 48-game block --
   **block voided**. Fixes: per-run class trees; refuse to recompile a tree in use; verify a predicate
   matches a live process before queueing on it.
5. **Silent fallback**: `vm-sync.sh` did not carry `progress/`, so `scrim.sh` fell back to a fixed
   roster for three days; "the user's rule ... was implemented, committed and never actually executed";
   57 of 65 ladder bots unplayed after 288 games.
6. **Reading a running batch as a result**: a 0-5 read that ended 11-5 -- one voided batch and a resumed
   gate.
7. **`pkill -f` with the pattern in your own command line** killed the issuing shell (exit 144), more
   than once ("twice more" in one incident alone). Use `[p]attern` or kill by PID after reading `/proc/<pid>/cmdline`. Killing `xargs` does not
   stop a batch runner; kill the script.
8. **`pgrep -c battlecode.server.Main` double-counts** (the `timeout` wrapper carries the class name).
9. **xargs children inherit the job pipe as stdin**; the engine reads its config from stdin (`-c=-`),
   so the first scan result was `unknown`. Give every game `</dev/null`.
10. **No wall-clock cap in the dev runner**: a diagnostic hung 83 minutes with no replay or log while task
    checks reported it running. `timeout` now lives in `run_game`.
11. **JIT code cache exhaustion** on heavy opponents (`compilation: disabled`) crawled games into the
    cap; `-XX:ReservedCodeCacheSize=512m`.
12. **Replay dumper OOM** at 256 MB on a 19 MB compressed replay aborted a whole block study; `-Xmx1g`.
13. **VM disk filled** (97% on 2026-09-22; 87% from another project's replays at the start). Losses are
    4-8 MB each; prune run directories after their study is fetched; check `df` before launching.
14. **Map files do not shorten games** (2021 engine ignored the map's round count) -- smoke tests cannot
    be shortened that way **[verify in 2023]**.
15. **`git checkout` will not revert a committed change**; restore from the snapshot directory with a
    package-line `sed` and diff to confirm.
16. **String replacement that silently matches nothing** -- whole iterations ran with edits missing;
    assert every substitution.
17. **Column misalignment in the metrics CSV** (header vs values) made "coverage" actually cumulative
    moves; a retraction built on it was itself wrong. Caught by the range check "coverage in [0, 1000]"
    reading 76,165.
18. **Map-size confounding and flattering denominators**: raw coverage counts; `--knowledge` using
    still-neutral + ever-sensed instead of neutrals at round 0 ("4 of 4, a perfect score" was the tell).
19. **Diagnostics on a map where the mechanism cannot fire, or can only fire at its best**: Iteration 32
    looked decisive on FindYourWay (four visible cheap neutrals) and lost 33-47 in the gate. "A candidate
    must be diagnosed on a map where the mechanism can fire *and* sanity-checked on one where it cannot."
20. **Tier table stale and unread** -> a rule breach (locked bot's replays reviewed). Rules enforced by
    memory fail; put them in tooling and fail closed.
21. **Archetypes that do not reproduce the condition, or cannot lose a game**: `arch_polrush` (eDanger
    0 all game), `arch_muck` (2 exposures vs rzhan11's 12-47), `arch_siege` (21/21 for both builds),
    `arch_expand` (8 centres to 0 and still lost on votes).
22. **Block-to-block comparison with a rotating pool** is meaningless; and exploration games inflate
    Elo (1511 -> 1692 from four weak newcomers).
23. **Official engine artefacts were dead**: the engine had to be built from source with two rotted
    dependencies patched.

## 8. Top 15 takeaways for the 2023 project (ranked by expected value)

1. **Gate with an SPRT mirror on random maps from the first candidate** (`mirror.sh` + `sprt.py`, p0 0.50
   / p1 0.58, alpha = beta = 0.05, batches of 16, cap 240) and use the stacking policy for inconclusive
   >= 53% results. 2021 lost a day and eight rejects to a fixed-cell panel that could only see 75%+
   effects.
2. **No test before one logged diagnostic game shows the pre-registered `@tag` counters firing** -- on a
   map where the mechanism *can* fire, plus a sanity map where it cannot. "A mechanism that compiles,
   runs, and logs nothing useful is the *normal* failure."
3. **Get the opening right before anything else.** 2021's biggest accepted effect (+22 points, 71.9%) and
   the first to move the ladder was spending the starting budget on income at round 1. For 2023, study
   the first 50 rounds (HQ build order, carrier count, first wells) against opponents' replays first
   **[inference]**.
4. **Look for binding constraints and silent blockers before inventing mechanisms**: every large accept
   removed a cap, a gate that fired on harmless enemies, a sink, or a jam. Count "wanted to build but
   could not" (no tile, no resources, gated) and "idle with resources" in the HQ log from day one.
5. **Instrument what each unit hears, not only what it does.** The broadcast-starvation bug survived four
   hypotheses and was found in one afternoon by six intake counters. In 2023, count shared-array writes
   attempted vs succeeded (write-range limits), overwrites, and what each reader actually decodes
   **[inference]**.
6. **Timestamp every shared-array claim** (wells, islands, enemy HQs, threats) and accept only newer
   ones; reserve bits for the stamp in the array layout before anything else is packed.
7. **Build the replay microscope for `.bc23` before the bot**: per-round team aggregates for both sides,
   event windows, per-unit tracks, our `@tag` logs, nav stats, bytecode. Port `ReplayDump`'s design; the
   schema must be rewritten.
8. **Mine every scrimmage block, wins included**: `scrim-study` -> `correlate` (within-opponent-and-map) ->
   `onset` (earliest riser at r200), then a diagnostic. Treat early-rising metrics as places to look;
   coverage and copying the opponent's allocation were markers, not levers.
9. **Self-play and the ladder disagree by design; pre-register which decides.** Distrust the mirror when a
   series of accepts never moves the rating; for defensive changes, pre-register a second arm against a
   sparring partner that reproduces the condition **and** wins 25-75% against the incumbent.
10. **Build sparring archetypes for each opponent behaviour that beats you, and run them before trusting
    them** (`arch_hunt`, `arch_lemon` were the most useful instruments 2021 had; four earlier ones were
    useless as built).
11. **Bytecode monitor from day one** (`over=`/`near=` per type every 50 turns, `@bcprof` per stage) and
    read it for every unit type in every logged game before trusting a gate result.
12. **Contest-realistic external play**: random map, random side, rotating opponents, Elo from our own
    games only, a **fixed established field** for like-for-like blocks, exploration reported separately.
13. **Infrastructure discipline**: games only on the VM (7 at a time on 8 vCPU), private class trees per
    run, `timeout` on every game, `</dev/null` for every engine, fail-closed guards instead of silent
    fallbacks, verified wait predicates, prune replays, check `df`.
14. **Unit-test the pure logic and the analysis pipeline**, including invariants between tuning constants
    and integrity ranges on live data; run after every bot or script change; verify the tests bite.
15. **Read the engine source for every fact you rely on, and re-verify 2021 facts in 3.0.15**: bytecode
    overrun behaviour, whether a unit built this round acts this round, turn order, sensing order (2021
    returned row-major order, a play-symmetry hazard -- pick best, not first), determinism and the
    final random tiebreak, instrumenter rejections of reflection-like calls, and whether map files can
    shorten games. "Eleven of the facts in section 5 contradicted what we assumed."
