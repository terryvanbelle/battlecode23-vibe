# Prior project: bc22 (Battlecode 2022 "Mutation", first attempt)

Reader report for the 2023 ("Tempest") project. Slice weight: lowest (the first attempt). Every prior document was read
from the filtered copy under `~/projects/vibe/reference/readroom-no2023/bc22/`. Code was read from
`~/projects/vibe/reference/battlecode22-vibe/`. Numbers are quoted as the sources wrote them. Anything I infer is marked
**(inference)**.

One-paragraph summary: over 6 days (2026-08-26 to 2026-09-01), an autonomous Claude Code loop ran 128 logged
iterations and kept 62 snapshots (`g_iter1..g_iter62`). The bot grew from a wandering Miner to a 1772-line
`RobotPlayer.java` plus a vendored 2388-line Dijkstra pathfinder. Measured against its own ancestors it rose to roughly
60-78% peer win rates. Against the two strong external bots it barely moved for the whole project:
`sample_camelcase` 0/20 in nearly every tally (best 1/20) and `sample_afinals` 2-4/20. The infrastructure (a VM
gauntlet, a replay-to-ASCII/metrics tool, game-by-game diffing) and the method lessons are worth much more than the bot.

## 2. What worked (with evidence and effect size)

The win rates below are "peer" rates: the current bot against its own frozen ancestors, 10 maps x 2 sides = 20 games per
opponent. That is a self-play yardstick (see section 4). The ordering is roughly by measured effect.

1. **Target enemy combat units before Archons (Iteration 28, `targetPriority`/`betterTarget`).** The old rule was
   "Archon first". The new tiers are combat units > Archon > workers, ties broken by lower HP. Evidence: the peer rate
   reached 203/260 = 78.1%, up from 62.1% in the preceding Gauntlet (previous best 69.4%). The mirror check gave 70%, against the usual
   45-55%. `pillars` went from 45% to 81%. It also produced the first win ever against `sample_camelcase` (1/20, sandwich,
   r296). Iteration 32 later put Laboratory at tier 2, above Archon (peer neutral). How it was found: an event-log trace
   of a stalled siege showed 4 of 5 Soldiers ignoring the defenders in front of them to honour "Archon first".

2. **A sticky Miner target (Iteration 35, `myLeadTarget`).** A Miner commits to one lead beacon until it arrives or the
   beacon drops below 6 lead, instead of recomputing "nearest" every round. Evidence: peer 251/360 = 69.7%, up from
   60.3% (+9.4 points), the largest single jump of the session. By map: intersection 44%->97%, jellyfish 62%->100%,
   squer 76%->100%, chessboard 35%->50%. On the reproduction game, mining actions went from 436 (vs 1253 for the
   opponent) to 1450 (vs 501), turning a loss into a win. How it was found: per-unit `--moves` traces showed Miners
   ping-ponging between two tiles for 10+ rounds. Aggregate stats had not revealed it.

3. **One shared army objective (Iteration 3).** Soldiers with nothing in sight all march to one point instead of "the
   mirror of my own position" (~85 distinct points). Priority: threatened own Archon > known enemy Archon > mirror of our
   first Archon. Evidence: 46/60 = 76.7%, and against `g_iter1` 16/20, recovering from Iteration 2's 5/20.

4. **Front-loaded economy (Iterations 4-7).** Build Miners straight to a cap, then pure army. The time-ramp
   interleave lost: `g_iter4` (flat cap 16) had 70 Soldiers by r150 to our 13. Iteration 6 reached 93/120 (78%) and
   lifted `g_iter4` from 6/20 to 13/20. Iteration 7 used a doctrine cap of about 10-16 Miners with Soldiers from about
   r30, copied from what every strong bot was observed doing. It added the `richHome` latch: on turn 1 the Archon
   senses more than 600 lead in vision and raises the Miner quota to 18. Result: highway and maze both went from 0/6 to
   6/6 against the ancestors, peer 91/120 = 75.8%.

5. **The vendored within-vision Dijkstra pathfinder (Iteration 8, `moveToward` -> `Dijkstra20.getBestDirection`).**
   Evidence: 13/20 against `g_iter7` on pathing alone, 14/20 with a narrow late-game Miner revival, peer 110/140 =
   78.6%. It was "the first time a change broke the first-mover symmetry", winning both sides on 5 maps. It replaced an
   8-direction greedy scorer that strung the army out on obstacle maps.

6. **A coherent combat package (Iteration 12).** It combined an accurate live census, `SA_FOCUS` shared focus fire, and
   retreat-to-heal at HP <= 15. Evidence: 13/20 against `g_iter9`, peer 124/160 = 77.5%, maptestsmall peer losses 8 -> 1.
   Each piece tested alone was inert or harmful (Iterations 8, 9 and 11, sections 3 and 7). Only the package worked.

7. **Archon repair (Iteration 14),** which made retreat-to-heal actually heal. Maze peer losses 9 -> 3, 12/20 against
   `g_iter10`, and wins as the second player on 6 maps. Iteration 91 later found its unbounded priority was a severe bug
   (item 13).

8. **Stable focus target (Iteration 24).** The census had been clearing `SA_FOCUS` every round, so reinforcing Soldiers
   chased a flickering point. Evidence: valley 9/22 (41%) -> 17/22 (77%). The diagnosis came from the `solSpread`
   metric: during a fight ours widened 8.3 -> 13.2 while the opponent's tightened 8.9 -> 4.0. Attack volume differed only
   1.19x, but losses differed 2.5x.

9. **Live fights over static guesses, and other priority-reachability fixes (Iterations 19, 37, 69/73).** Idle Soldiers
   now read `SA_FOCUS` (reinforce the live fight) before the static objective. Sampled turns had been 62% "objective"
   and 9% "focus". `SA_HOME_THREAT` and the raided-Miner cry (`SA_ECON_THREAT`) were moved ahead of reinforcement,
   because both had been structurally unreachable whenever any fight existed. The raid response finally shipped with a
   self-calibrating throttle (Iteration 73): it counts distinct raid events per round/50 bucket and responds only while
   the count is 3 or less. Result 169/280 = 60.4%, with every one of 14 opponents equal or better. Four fixed-distance
   gates had failed before it (Iterations 69-72).

10. **The multi-Archon fairness yield (Iteration 61).** An Archon that just built a Soldier yields for 6 rounds, but
    only when `curArchons > 1` and a sibling has broadcast "hungry" within the last 8 rounds. Result: 195/300 = 65.0%,
    which closed a 23-iteration thread (39-61). The root cause was turn-order dominance over the shared lead pool
    (whoever acts first takes any contested surplus), not aggregate income.

11. **Exploration momentum with a strict stuck detector (Iteration 86, `moveExplore`).** Keep a heading, re-roll it with
    probability 1/15 or when blocked, and if net displacement over 10 rounds is at most 2 tiles (dist² <= 4), random-walk
    for 10 rounds. Evidence: 244/380 = 64.2% against 209/360 = 58.1%. valley 15/36 -> 23/36, pillars 17/36 -> 26/36.
    Before the fix, about 64% of Miner turns on one valley loss (about 4900 of 7700) were spent in the pure random walk.
    v1 trapped Miners (one made 111 moves for 4.5 tiles of net progress) and v2 false-triggered. The strictest version
    is the one that worked.

12. **Symmetric tie-breaking (Iteration 126, shipped on owner direction).** The fix replaced 8 fixed-compass `DIRS`
    tie-break sites with "toward map centre" or "toward the goal/target". `betterTarget` now breaks exact ties by
    distance instead of engine scan order, and so does the Miner's richest-tile search. Result: 439/720 = 61.0%, against
    a 414/720 = 57.5% baseline. The A/B side gap fell from 27.8 to 14.2 points (A 68.1%, B 53.9%). maptestsmall went from
    100% B-side losses to 33%. Two other maps were still sharply asymmetric (chessboard 81%/0% B/A losses, squer 89%/19%).
    The fix was a net gain only once the `betterTarget` change was included. The 8-site fix alone scored 330/720 = 45.8%
    (section 3).

13. **Real bug fixes found by tracing benchmark losses (Iterations 91-94).**
    - Heal-vs-build (91): heal pre-empted build for any unit missing more than 6 HP, with no bound. In one game, team
      lead hit 6815 unspent by r180 while Soldiers crashed 36 -> 0. Fix: if lead is above 600, only units below 20% HP
      pre-empt a build. Lead peak fell to 4791, and the peer rate held at 275/440 = 62.5%.
    - Builder flee (92): `foes` had been unused.
    - Builder replacement (93): a spawned-ever counter never decremented.
    - Builder anti-freeze (94): one Builder sat frozen for 800+ rounds.

    All verified clean at full scale. The peer win-rate effect was near zero, but these were genuine correctness fixes.

14. **Structural "bold" changes gated on a stable map-level signal (Iterations 109, 110, 115).** A 4x Builder cap on
    `richHome` maps only: ungated, v1 caused a 20-game one-directional collapse on `highway` across 12 opponents; gated,
    v2 had zero diffs (374/640). A higher Miner ceiling (`6 + round/30`, cap 50) on `richHome` only: zero diffs. Removing
    the dead `recentEnemyContact` gate on Sage production: 1 isolated diff in 700 games. The log's own lesson, from the
    failed ungated Iteration 113: "the gate is what makes the pattern safe, not the specific number."

15. **Escalating surplus thresholds for discretionary spending (Iterations 117-125).** Extra Watchtowers per Builder at
    1000/2500/5000 lead, and a 2nd and 3rd Laboratory at 1500/3000. All had zero peer diffs, but they measurably
    extended survival against camelcase on its motivating game: r255 -> r513 (117) -> r597 (118). None changed a
    benchmark tally.

16. **Live bytecode monitoring (Iteration 127, `checkBytecodeBudget`).** If the round number changed between the start
    of the turn and the `finally` block, the turn was truncated (an overrun). Separately, usage of 85% or more of the
    budget counts as a near miss. Two shared counters surface both in the Archon indicator string.
    `tools/check_bytecode.py` scans replays in bulk. The log reported 0 near misses and 0 overruns in two stress games.
    LEARNINGS later reports "2 confirmed in a 720-game competitive Gauntlet" in the longest, most unit-dense games.
    Status: "monitored, not yet mitigated". Earlier, a one-off profiler run had found worst-case usage of about 48-50%
    (Miner max 4968/10000).

17. **Infrastructure that worked:** bare `java` per game in 6-way parallel on an 8-vCPU VM; plain ssh with the gcloud
    key instead of `gcloud compute ssh`; `bc22_replay.py --metrics/--indicators/--moves/--all-actions`; game-by-game diffs
    via `compare_gauntlets.py` and matched-subset comparison; a fixed old-snapshot yardstick (`vs_old_bots_history.csv`).
    Details in section 6.

## 3. What did not work, and closed directions

### The headline failure: the benchmark gap never closed

- **`sample_camelcase`** (a real 2022 finalist, "13th-16th at finals") was 0/20 in nearly every tally across all 128
  iterations, with a best of 1/20 (Iterations 28-34). Average losing-game length was flat at about 340-370 rounds for the
  whole project (`benchmark_game_length.png`).
- **`sample_afinals`** ("Most Adaptive Strategy" prize) stayed in a 2/20 to 4/20 band. The final tallies, after
  Iterations 115-126, were 3/20 five checks in a row.
- **`sample_monke`** (lecture-level) was 20/20.
- **The fixed old-bot yardstick says the same.** `g_iter56` and `g_iter58` against the old snapshots: `g_iter1` 100%,
  `g_iter11` 80%, `g_iter21` 55%, `g_iter31` 60%, `g_iter41` 50%, `g_iter51` 50%. **(inference)** After about Iteration
  28 (`g_iter21`), some 35 accepted snapshots added little measurable absolute strength. Many were accepted as "safe,
  verified, currently-neutral" with zero diffs (Iterations 78, 81, 82, 83, 85, 92, 93, 94, 96, 97, 116, 117, 120, 122,
  123, 125).
- **The diagnosis the log converged on** (LEARNINGS, Iterations 100/101/122): a production-scale and doctrine
  mismatch, not a chain of tactical bugs. Camelcase ran a continuous 3:1:1 SOLDIER:MINER:BUILDER spawn cycle and reached
  72 Miners and 143 Watchtowers on a 1-Archon map. Afinals ran 4-6 Labs, built 111 Sages and zero Soldiers, and banked
  311 gold, while our gold sat at 0 all game. "Raw mass frequently beats accumulated economic sophistication": every
  infrastructure build is a build turn not spent on a Soldier, and the single-Archon throughput is about 1 build per 2
  rounds.

### Closed threads (evidence, and why each failed)

- **Economy-knob tuning (Iterations 5, 7, 13, 16, 17, 23, 26, 62).** Every Miner-count formula fixed one cluster of
  maps and broke another. A flat team target of 9 gave camelcase survival r389 but cost 8 peer wins. A size-gated version
  cost 11. A steep floor (`6 + r/50`, cap 30) scored 56.8% and dropped `g_iter14-16` to 40-45%. Peak-triggered boosts
  scored 58.8% and 59.2%. Conclusion: the opening Miner count is "map-dependent in a way no single formula captures".
  What finally shipped was signal-gated (`richHome`) or self-replacing via the census.

- **Isolated micro changes on top of an army that never accumulated (Iterations 8-11).** Retreat, kiting, focus fire
  and rally each landed at 10-11/20 against `g_iter7`, "measurably zero effect". The census bug fix alone regressed to
  52.9%, because `g_iter7`'s build rule had been tuned around the buggy cumulative counter. Lesson: when downstream logic
  is calibrated around a bug, fix the bug and re-tune together.

- **Fixed garrisons and standing guards (Iterations 9, 13, 40).** A 1/4 or 1/5 ID-based garrison split the small early
  army (worse, about 2/11; and 8/20). A per-Archon standing guard scored a 30% mirror: it is useless on 1-Archon maps and
  always costs a unit.

- **Sandwich Archon starvation (Iterations 39-61, 23 iterations).** Iterations 39-44 were six inference-only diagnoses
  (richHome? fleeing Miners? ring crowding? rubble?). The cause turned out to be shared-lead turn-order contention, found
  only once per-round debug values were printed. A greedy "build a Miner if a Soldier is unaffordable" fallback gave a
  **1/20 mirror** (5%). A patient version gave 30%. Income-side fixes never moved the starved Archon at all. The
  fairness-yield variants scored 54.0%, 53.3%, 58.7% and 59.3% before the gated 6-round version reached 65.0%.

- **Watchtower scaling as an isolated lever (Iterations 54-56, 68).** Four variants produced the identical small,
  neutral per-opponent signature. Forward placement produced zero flips across 60 games. Conclusion: the camelcase
  Watchtower doctrine "only pays off as part of a broader, integrated strategy".

- **The Laboratory placed on the Archon's ring (Iteration 57).** Catastrophic: 9750 lead unspent, Soldiers 38 -> 0, gold
  0 all game. Permanent structures in the 8-tile build ring block `canBuildRobot` (an occupancy check, confirmed by
  `javap`).

- **The gold economy against afinals, supply side (Iterations 64, 84, 89, 90, 98, plus threshold tests).** Passive gold
  detection never fired: 4907 "no gold known" hits and 0 "seeking gold". Active gold scouts (10% of Miners) worked
  mechanically and produced the first gold ever mined (17), but scored 95/160, net -10. Faster Builder production made
  more victims: all 4 Builders were killed by Sages. The thread closed as "survival, not supply". Sage `actionRadiusSquared`
  25 exceeds Builder/Miner/Soldier vision 20, so Builders die to threats they cannot see.

- **Symmetry detection (Iterations 76, 87, 87v2, 88).** The blind rotational guess `(W-1-x, H-1-y)` was provably wrong
  on 4 of 10 loop maps: maze, intersection and jellyfish are horizontal, highway is vertical.
  - Reactive detection never triggered (the wrong guess prevented contact).
  - Time-cycling scored 98/160 against 100/160.
  - Miner reporting of enemy Archons: 103/160 against 105/160. Adding the mass-gate fix gave zero diffs.
  - A correct terrain-based scout (one Miner samples rubble at 2 points near the centre and their 3 mirrors; it
    resolved `maze` in about 26 rounds): 96/160 against 105/160, with 39 flips.

  Why: the scout's economic cost is paid on every map, the 6/10 rotational maps gain nothing, and correct information
  sometimes triggered worse early commitments. The thread was closed. **(inference for 2023)** The lesson is "make
  detection free", not "never detect" (section 5).

- **Fixed-compass tie-break fixes before the owner overrode (Iterations 67 and 126 v1-v3).** The decorrelation and
  random tie-break variants scored 50.0%, 54.4%, 58.8% and 59.4% (sample sizes vary). A per-robot shuffled `DIRS` left
  a 23.75-point A/B gap. The explanation offered: a consistent shared directional preference gives "movement cohesion"
  that the other tuning had adapted to. The version that shipped (section 2, item 12) needed the `betterTarget` distance
  tie-break as well.

- **Soldier rally and mass gate before reinforcing (Iterations 25, 33, 104 v1/v2, 107, 108).** 52.5% and 59.7% on the
  early variants. Later variants fixed chessboard and pillars but regressed `highway`, which consistently rewards a
  spread army. Discriminators tried: army size, cumulative attack density, windowed attack density. None protected
  highway. Closed as "a genuinely open question about what distinguishes highway".

- **Home-defence distance comparison (Iteration 102).** 56.6%, with 15 diffs, all win -> loss, concentrated on squer/B
  across 9 opponents. On small-army maps (2-11 Soldiers in total), "defend only if home is closer" strips home bare.
  Iteration 103 shipped a conservative version gated on a team-wide `SA_SOLDIERS >= 10`, which gave up the original
  benefit.

- **Miner target diversion floors (Iterations 77 v1-v3, 79, 105, 106).** Absolute stickiness scored 68/160. Hysteresis
  margins: 88/160. A self-calibrating escalating margin: 83/160. A minimum-lead floor on the vision branch produced 56 and
  53 diffs at the 8-peer scale. Closed: "this branch itself (executed by every Miner, every round, starting round 1) is
  fundamentally too early and too frequently-executed for any quick single-condition tweak".

- **The repair-vs-build throttle and the distress signal (Iterations 63, 74, 75).** 60.6% raw but 65.0 -> 61.0 on the
  shared pool, with 14 of 16 flips unfavourable; then 59/100 against 60/100; then 54/100. The real bottleneck was
  Soldier survival in isolated skirmishes: 100% mortality against about 43% for the opponent.

- **Never-engaging fixes:** a Sage pause to reach Archon level 3 (gold never exceeded 64), a "losing war" consolidation
  (0 hits; attrition happens in direct combat), multi-Archon threat severity (no competing threat in the motivating
  game), and the first Sage kiting attempt (0 Sages existed; it was accepted later, as Iteration 116, with zero diffs).

## 4. Method lessons

### 4.1 The training loop as written (`TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md`)

- **The Gauntlet** is every opponent x every board x both sides. In practice it was a 10-map "loop" set. The log header
  says 14 maps, but `gauntlet.sh` uses 10. That gives 20 games per opponent.
- **Peer vs benchmark split.** A peer is an opponent we beat 30-90% of the time; a benchmark is one we beat less than
  30%. The accept gate counts peer games only. Benchmarks are played every `BenchmarkEvery = 3` Gauntlets.
- **Retirement.** A bot we beat 80% or more (originally 90%) in two consecutive Gauntlets leaves the pool.
- **Hyperparameters:** `WinPct = 60%`, `MaxHypothesisIterations` and `MaxSolutionsIterations` raised from 5 to 10,
  `ReproSampleSize = 8` peers, `NearMissMargin = 5`, `MaxNearMissRefinements = 3`, `MaxConsecutiveRejects = 3`. There was
  also an earlier `MirrorCheckMinWinPct = 35%`.
- **The step loop:** pick a losing game, form a hypothesis with explicit variable/threshold tables (verified against the
  replay), run a generality check on a second losing game, then an "act-on-it" check (don't revert documented earlier
  reasoning).
- **Three-outcome mechanistic verification:** the game is won; or still lost but the fix engaged, with an evidenced
  account of why it couldn't flip; or no evidence (undo). Then the 8-peer reproduction sample, then the full Gauntlet,
  read by the shape of the diff.
- **High-risk structural exploration** is a first-class track. It fires after 3 consecutive rejects, a fruitless Step 4,
  or a stale bold idea. **Never idle.**
- **Iteration 0 must include** replay instrumentation and live bytecode monitoring.

### 4.2 Gating and statistics: what the project learned the hard way

- **The engine is deterministic for fixed code.** Iteration 53 re-ran identical code and got "byte-for-byte identical"
  178/300 with matching per-opponent numbers. Iteration 119 reran a game twice and got the identical r1435. One note
  (Iteration 116) claims reruns were "not perfectly seed-stable". That is unresolved, so verify determinism on the 2023
  engine on day one. **(inference)** Consequence: "noise" between two code versions is not sampling noise. It is chaotic
  sensitivity to any perturbation (a butterfly effect over hundreds of rounds), and the only way to average it out is
  more distinct games (maps x sides x opponents).

- **Per-opponent numbers at n = 20 are mostly noise.** A 5-point move is one game. The binomial SD at n = 20 and p = 0.5
  is about 11 points **(inference)**. The log computed about 2.8% at n = 300. Early iterations over-read shifts like
  "g_iter16 -5, g_iter17/18 +5".

- **Shape-of-diff heuristic** (written into the algorithm after Iteration 102):
  - Likely noise: a few diffs, scattered across maps and opponents, mixed in direction, on known-fragile maps
    (chessboard, squer, sandwich, maptestsmall, pillars).
  - Likely real: one-directional, and/or concentrated on one map and side across many opponents. Example: Iteration 102
    had 15 diffs, all win -> loss, on squer/B across 9 opponents.

- **Always diff against the current accepted baseline's own full run,** keyed on (opponent, map, side). Retire the
  "stale baseline plus list of known flips" practice. Iterations 70-72 fell victim to a **wrong-baseline error**:
  `g_iter19` was compared against `g_iter21`'s baseline.

- **No-op control test (Iteration 59).** Three unrelated Builder changes produced identical per-opponent numbers. An
  inert one-line change reproduced the original baseline instead, which falsified "any code change shifts results" and
  narrowed the effect to Builder timing.

- **Eight-peer samples miss concentrated regressions.** In Iteration 91 the 8-peer sample showed 1 flip; the full
  Gauntlet showed 6, all `highway`/B. In 103 v2 the sample showed 2 squer flips; the full run showed 9. In 111 the sample
  showed 1 flip; the full run found 5, all `maptestsmall`/A. Rule: changes to economy timing, thresholds or priority
  orderings get full-scale verification. The other direction also holds: a huge small-sample diff (52-56 diffs at 8
  peers) justifies an early abort.

- **The aggregate gate drifts.** As the roster filled with near-identical recent siblings (about 50% each by
  construction), the raw peer aggregate drifted from about 65% to 57.5% "independent of actual quality". Iterations
  99-125 were accepted at 57.5-59.8%, below the literal 60% bar, by judging the diff on the older-peer subset. **The 60%
  absolute gate stopped meaning anything.**

- **Round-count delta** (`compare_gauntlets.py`) is a secondary signal: wins faster or losses slower count as progress.
  It was used to prove a change "isn't dead code" when no outcome flipped.

- **"Helps the case it was diagnosed on, hurts broadly"** recurred (Iterations 63, 69, 74, 75, 76). Before
  implementing, measure how often the trigger condition fires across other recent games.

- **Gates that never toggle.** "No contact in N rounds" is permanently false in a sustained war. Hit in Iterations 20,
  30, 114 and 115, and in the `SA_HOME_THREAT` never-clears bug (Iteration 37). Verify a gate toggles in a real long game
  before trusting it.

### 4.3 Ladder and Elo design

There was **no Elo or rating ladder.** "Progress" was cumulative accepted snapshots (the cumulative-iterations chart),
peer WinPct, best/worst peer spread, benchmark tallies out of 20, and a fixed old-bot roster (every 10th snapshot) added
late, at `g_iter56`.

Weaknesses **(inference, from the evidence above)**:
- The peer pool is self-play against one lineage. All peers shared the same `DIRS` bias, so a fairness fix looked like a
  regression against them (Iteration 67).
- Retirement makes the pool's difficulty drift.
- The absolute gate drifts (4.2).
- Benchmark tallies ran only every 3 Gauntlets, and the owner noticed none had run for a long stretch (ART_OF_WAR:
  "no full benchmark tally had been run in the entire visible session until the user asked for one"). The first such
  tally immediately exposed the 6815-lead heal bug.

For 2023 **(inference)**: use a rating ladder that includes external bots, plus a fixed yardstick roster that never
retires, plus paired game-by-game diffs against the current baseline as the accept gate.

### 4.4 Process rules that stuck

- Archive the single most interesting replay per iteration into `replays/` in the same commit. It "fell behind for 14
  straight iterations" while it was a separate step.
- Track functional areas, not just games, so a run of rejects reads as one exhausted thread.
- `MaxConsecutiveRejects = 3` bars a 4th attempt in the same area.
- Read opponent source before hypothesizing about it. That is forbidden for external bots in 2023, so watch their
  replays and metrics instead.
- Snapshot through `tools/snapshot.sh` (a package rewrite).
- The algorithm document was rewritten mid-run to match practice, after the owner asked for it.

2. **Iteration 26:** archive one replay per iteration in git (process).
3. **Iteration 33:** "Don't worry so much when you try a big idea... we're never going to defeat camelcase by being

5. **Iteration 61:** accepted the `g_iter22-26` timing-sensitivity trade-off and told the agent to keep refining.
6. **Iteration 91:** asked for the benchmark win rate. Tracing that tally found the heal-priority lead-hoarding bug

7. **After Iteration 102:** asked for the rewrite of `TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md` to formalize what actually worked
   (mechanistic verification, shape-based diffs, a maintained baseline). Earlier the owner had rejected a

8. **Iteration 108:** prompted one more attempt. **After 108:** "not working on anything is not an acceptable response";

## 5. Bot architecture and the basics, and how each maps onto 2023

**Layout.** `src/bot/RobotPlayer.java` holds everything: one `run()` loop, then `census()`, an enemy-sighting stamp, a
Sage report, `reportEnemyArchons`, `checkHomeThreat`, and dispatch to `runArchon`, `runMiner`, `runSoldier` (Soldier and
Sage), `runBuilder`, `runWatchtower` and `runLaboratory`. The loop wraps everything in try/catch, and its `finally`
block calls `checkBytecodeBudget` and then `Clock.yield()`. Robot state lives in static fields, which are per-robot
(LEARNINGS: "Static fields are per-robot instance... genuine team-wide coordination requires the shared array").

**Shared-array map** (64 x 16-bit slots; `pack(l) = 1 + x*64 + y`, so 0 means empty). This also fits 2023's 60x60:
the maximum is 3836.

| 3, 4 | `SA_ATTACKS`, `SA_BUILDS` | Cumulative counters (`bump`, capped at 65535) |

### 5.1 Economy

- **Techniques.**
  - `runArchon` latches `richHome` (more than 600 lead in vision on turn 1) and sets the per-Archon quota
    (`richHome ? 18 : max(leadTiles in 3x3, 5)`).
  - The contact cut drops the quota to 3 when an enemy was seen within 60 rounds and the spawn is not `richHome`.
  - The ongoing floor is `min(6 + r/100, 25)`, or on `richHome` maps `max(18, min(6 + r/30, 50))`. It is suppressed
    under `localThreat` (a foe within dist² 40).
  - Build priority: Sage if gold >= 20, then Builder (gated on round, Miners >= 8 and lead > 120), then Miner, then
    Soldier.
  - Heal-vs-build is lead-aware.
  - Miners mine the 3x3 ring (gold first, then lead down to 1 for regeneration), flee threats while crying for help, go
    to the richest visible tile (distance tie-break), publish beacons, and commit to a sticky beacon.
- **2023 mapping (inference).**
  - The single-build-action ceiling that dominated 2022 does not exist in 2023: the HQ can build several units per turn.
    Production is bounded by income (carrier trips, well throughput and the passive +6 Ad / +6 Mn every 5 rounds). So
    the 2022 doctrine finding ("raw mass beats sophistication; every infrastructure build is a unit not built") maps to
    "maximize resource income early and convert it to launchers continuously". Do not hoard.
  - The `richHome` pattern maps to latching per-HQ well counts and types (Ad/Mn) in HQ vision on turn 1, and branching
    the carrier quota on it.
  - The sticky-target lesson (+9.4 points) applies directly to carriers choosing a well and an HQ: commit, don't
    re-pick "nearest" every round.
  - The build-ring occupancy trap maps to carriers crowding well-adjacent tiles and HQ deposit tiles. Measure queueing.
    Check the HQ spawn radius in `RULES.md` before assuming spawns can be blocked.
  - "Escalating surplus thresholds for discretionary spending" maps to anchors (80 Ad + 80 Mn) and amplifiers: commit
    the first, then gate further ones on an increasing surplus bar.
  - The heal-vs-build bug class becomes "any unconditional-priority action that can starve production". Audit every
    priority chain for an upper bound.

### 5.2 Navigation and pathing

- **Techniques.** `moveToward` asks `Dijkstra20.getBestDirection(goal, blocked)` for a step, where `blocked` is the
  opposite of `lastDir` if the goal is unchanged. Dijkstra20 is an unrolled within-vision (r² <= 20) shortest path with
  cost `1 + rubble`. If the step is blocked, the unit side-steps (`rotateLeft`/`rotateRight`, the Iteration 29 congestion
  fix). Otherwise a greedy 8-direction scorer runs: `-sqrt(dist) - 0.02 * rubble`, plus 0.6 to keep the heading and 0.3
  for a 45-degree turn, with a centre-distance tie-break. `repositionForRubble` steps onto a lower-rubble tile within
  attack range before firing (movement and action are separate cooldowns).
- **Known limits.** Dijkstra's internal tie-break order is a fixed generated chain (31+ candidates), never audited. It
  is a suspected residual source of side bias, and bypassing it made the A/B gap no better (22.5 points).
- **2023 mapping (inference).** No rubble. Instead walls, clouds (vision r² 4, cooldowns +20%), and currents that push
  units at end of turn. The Dijkstra idea ports if the cost model becomes: wall = impassable, cloud = penalty, current =
  displacement-aware (a current tile pushes you, so its effective cost depends on direction). The generator approach (a
  Python script that emits unrolled code, per the vendored header's "Generated by scripts/dijkstra.py") is the reusable
  idea. Write our own generator rather than vendoring. Make sure every tie-break is symmetric, which the 2022 generated
  chain was not. "Reposition to lower rubble before firing" becomes "step out of a cloud or current before or after
  firing". The "prefer axis-aligned moves on ties to keep formation" principle (RESEARCH.md) is worth testing on
  launchers with move cooldown 20.

### 5.3 Exploration

- **Techniques.** `moveExplore`: a persistent heading re-rolled on block or with 1/15 chance per round, and a stuck
  detector (10-round window, net dist² <= 4) that triggers 10 rounds of random walk. Lead beacons in the shared array
  stopped idle Miners random-walking near spawn on lead-sparse maps. Active gold scouting was too expensive.
- **2023 mapping (inference).** Wells are sparse fixed points and islands are fixed regions, so exploration should
  publish them: the well/island equivalent of beacons, written whenever in write range. The momentum plus strict
  stuck-detector design ports directly. RESEARCH.md's repulsion-field explorers with momentum (wololo, 2021) are another
  documented option. The cost lesson from Iteration 98 (permanently diverting 10% of the workforce lost 10 net games):
  scouting must piggyback on units that are moving anyway.

### 5.4 Symmetry

- **The 2022 state.** `armyObjective` assumed rotational symmetry (wrong on 4 of 10 loop maps). The replay header has a
  `symmetry` field (rotational/horizontal/vertical) for offline checks. The proactive terrain scout was correct but net
  negative.
- **Play symmetry**, the bigger finding. Fixed `DIRS` iteration order, first-of-scan picks over `senseNearbyRobots` and
  `senseNearbyLocationsWithLead` results (engine scan order is absolute), and a shared fixed-seed RNG all produce
  map-dependent side bias. Identical code favoured A on some maps and B on others (6-4 across 10 maps).
- **Residual issue in the shipped bot (inference, not tested in the log).** `moveExplore` still draws from
  `static final Random rng = new Random(6147)`. Statics are per robot, so every robot's RNG starts at the same seed and
  draws absolute directions. LEARNINGS flags exactly this hazard ("a naive attempt to use it for decorrelating... can
  silently reproduce the exact same bug").
- **2023 mapping (inference).** Every robot knows all own HQ locations at round 1, so the enemy HQ candidates are the 3
  transforms. Sensing whether a candidate tile holds an enemy HQ, or comparing sensed `MapInfo` (walls, clouds, currents,
  wells, islands) at a tile and its mirror, eliminates hypotheses as a side effect of ordinary launcher and carrier
  movement. That is the "free" detection Iteration 88 lacked. RESEARCH.md adds: check only the informative half, and
  send scouts toward the centre. Write the result whenever in write range. From day one, also:
  - express every tie-break relative to goal, target or map centre, or seed it from `rc.getID()`;
  - never take the first match of a scan;
  - run a self-mirror match on every map each iteration and track per-map, per-side win rates.

### 5.5 Combat and micro

- **Techniques.**
  - `targetPriority`: combat 3 > Lab 2 > Archon 1 > workers 0; then lowest HP; then distance.
  - `SA_FOCUS` promotion and dead-target clearing.
  - Retreat when HP <= 10 (Sage: 30) and not near home; Archons repair.
  - Reinforce the live focus before the speculative objective.
  - Mass gate: during rounds 60-250, wait for 3 friendly combat units before advancing toward a speculative objective.
  - Home defence first, unless the army is big enough (`SA_SOLDIERS >= 10`) and the fight is closer.
  - Raided-Miner rescue with a throttle.
  - Sage-only overkill avoidance and "standoff" kiting (exploiting Sage action r² 25 > others' vision r² 20).
- **Quantified findings.**
  - Retreating at HP <= 15 anywhere made 28% of sampled turns "heal" and 7% attacking. Raising the threshold to <= 10
    extended camelcase games r161 -> r188.
  - Combat "trade-efficiency gaps" usually traced upstream: kills were even, 10 against 9, until an Archon-count
    divergence.
  - Isolated units die at 100% against about 43% for the grouped opponent.
  - Formation spread (`solSpread`) predicted fight outcomes on combat-dense maps but not on `highway`.
- **2023 mapping (inference).**
  - The launcher attack r² 16 is smaller than every unit's vision r² 20, so there is no hidden-attack gap between
    launchers. The analogue to check is clouds: a unit inside a cloud sees only r² 4. Verify in the engine whether
    attacking into or out of clouds is allowed.
  - Target priority "combat first" maps to launchers (and destabilizers) over carriers. Carriers may still be the
    economic kill: RESEARCH.md calls unit count a currency, and denial of production is cheaper than direct combat.
  - The HQ deals 4 damage per round within r² 9 and is indestructible. A siege is therefore about denying wells, islands
    and carriers, not killing the HQ.
  - Retreat thresholds should be proportional to max HP (launcher 200).
  - Shared focus fire across the map is not possible without amplifiers. Use deterministic local target choice (same
    priority, HP, distance and ID rules on every unit) so nearby launchers converge without communication.
  - The mass-gate lesson matters more with a move cooldown of 20: arrive together.

### 5.6 Communication

- **Design principles that worked:** fixed purpose slots, one writer discipline, an explicit clearing condition for
  every flag, and freshness rounds stored alongside locations.
- **What failed repeatedly:** flags whose clear condition never occurs ("no contact recently"), and priority chains in
  which a correctly raised flag is unreachable.
- **RESEARCH.md adds:** batch writes through a local copy with dirty flags (a buffer pool), and log-encode wide-range
  quantities.
- **2023 mapping (inference).** Writes are allowed only within r² 9 of an own HQ, r² 20 of an own amplifier, or r² 4 of
  an own anchored island. Reads are unrestricted. So:
  - The 2022 census ("every unit bumps an accumulator every round") does not port. A live census needs HQ-side counting
    (spawns) plus heartbeats written when units pass HQs or amplifiers, or slot aging.
  - `SA_FOCUS` only works inside amplifier coverage.
  - Stale-flag risk is higher, because a flag set near the HQ may not be clearable by the unit that later sees the
    situation resolved. Every slot needs a round stamp and a time-to-live.
  - Treat amplifiers as the comms network. Their placement is a design decision, not an afterthought.

### 5.7 Bytecode, robustness and instrumentation

- **Techniques.** `checkBytecodeBudget(rc, roundAtStart)`:
  - If the round number changed, the turn was truncated: count an overrun.
  - Otherwise, if `Clock.getBytecodeNum() * 100 >= bytecodeLimit * 85`, count a near miss.
  - The check sits in its own try/catch so monitoring cannot crash the robot.
  - The Archon writes a compact indicator string: `r.. bcN.. bcO.. L.. G.. A.. HP.. M.. S.. atk.. eA.. HT..`. It was
    compacted after silent truncation was discovered: the old string had been cut mid-word ("atta").
  - Any exception is caught and printed. The loop never dies.
  - Per-unit indicator strings ("focus", "reinforce", "heal", "defend home", "mass N") made `--indicators` turn-state
    histograms possible. That was the single most productive diagnostic.
- **2023 mapping (inference).** It ports almost verbatim. Budgets are HQ 20000, carrier 12500, others 10000. Confirm
  with `javap` that `RobotType.bytecodeLimit` and `Clock.getBytecodeNum()` exist in 3.0.15. Write the counters only when
  in write range, or keep them per-robot and print them. Put the counters first in the indicator string, and test the
  string length limit on 3.0.15 directly.

## 8. Top 15 takeaways for 2023, ranked by expected value

1. **Measure against strong, independent opponents and a fixed yardstick from the start.** Gate on paired game-by-game
   diffs against the current baseline, not on an absolute peer win rate. 62 accepted snapshots moved camelcase from
   0/20 to, at best, 1/20; afinals stayed at 2-4/20; and the latest bot beat `g_iter21` only 55%. Self-play against one
   lineage measured mostly noise and drift after about Iteration 28.

2. **Get the doctrine and scale right before micro.** The benchmark gap was production scale: 72 Miners and 143
   Watchtowers, or 111 Sages, against our trickle. In 2023 the HQ is not build-action-limited, so income is the ceiling
   **(inference)**. Optimize carrier income, well choice and launcher conversion first. Watch strong opponents' replays
   for unit counts over time, through the `--metrics` port.

3. **Port the replay tool first** (`bc22_replay.py` -> bc23: `--metrics`, `--indicators`, `--moves`, ASCII board).
   Nearly every root cause in 2022 came from tracing one replay: per-unit move traces, state histograms of indicator
   strings, and attack-rate or formation-spread metrics. Aggregate tables did not find them.

4. **Play symmetry as a day-one invariant.** No fixed `Direction[]` order for tie-breaks, no first-of-scan picks,
   per-robot RNG seeded from the ID, a self-mirror match on every map every iteration, and per-map, per-side win-rate
   tables. Fixing this in 2022 gave +3.5 points and halved the A/B gap (27.8 -> 14.2), after about 60 iterations of
   unknowingly tuning around the bias.

5. **Live bytecode, exception and indicator-length monitoring from Iteration 0.** Port `checkBytecodeBudget`
   (round-change overrun detection, 85% near-miss) and a bulk scanner. Overruns appeared only later, in long,
   unit-dense games.

6. **Sticky commitments beat recomputing "nearest" every round.** Commit carriers to wells and HQs, and launchers to
   targets and rally points, until reached or invalid. This was the largest single jump (+9.4 points) in 2022.

7. **Design comms around 2023's write ranges** **(inference)**. Make amplifiers the network. Give every slot a round
   stamp, a time-to-live, a single owner and an explicit clear rule. Replace the global census and focus slot with
   HQ-side accounting and deterministic local target choice. The 2022 bus design (purpose slots, `pack = 1 + x*64 + y`)
   ports; its "every unit writes every round" assumptions do not.

8. **Verify every gate actually toggles, and every priority branch is reachable,** in long real games. "No contact
   recently" never clears in a real war (4+ dead mechanisms). A home-threat or raid flag behind the "reinforce live
   fight" branch was unreachable for most of a game (Iterations 37 and 69).

9. **Basic combat rules that paid off.** One shared objective, not per-unit objectives (62.5% -> 76.7%). Reinforce the
   live fight. Gather 3 or more before a speculative advance. Shoot combat units first (62.1% -> 78.1%). Retreat only at critical
   HP, proportional to max HP (the 28% "heal" vs 7% "attack" finding). Keep the focus target stable (valley 41% -> 77%).

10. **Symmetry detection must be free.** A correct dedicated scout was net -9 games in 2022. In 2023, eliminate
    candidate symmetries passively from known own-HQ positions and sensed terrain or wells along normal unit paths, and
    publish the result when in range **(inference)**.

11. **Statistics discipline for a deterministic engine.** "Noise" is chaotic perturbation, not sampling. Read diffs by
    shape (one-directional or concentrated means real; scattered and mixed means noise). Use matched subsets. Run a
    no-op control when results look suspiciously shared. Early-abort on huge diffs. Run full scale for economy and
    threshold changes. Never trust per-opponent n = 20 moves.

12. **Decompile and sweep the engine** (`javap` on `battlecode23-3.0.15.jar`). Resolve rules doubts before shelving or
    guessing. Periodically diff the full `RobotController` method list against methods we actually call. In 2022 this
    found transform, mutate and the gold APIs late, and settled `canBuildRobot` semantics.

13. **Gate bold changes on a stable map-level signal, or self-calibrate them from in-game counts.** Avoid fixed
    constants. Ungated bold changes collapsed specific maps (highway, a 20-game sweep; 52 diffs). The `richHome`-gated
    versions had zero cost. The raid-count throttle was the only fix that satisfied all 14 opponents.

14. **Infrastructure that is known to work:** bare `java` per game, parallel jobs, plain ssh, a `setsid` runner with
    polling, the `results.csv` schema, `compare_gauntlets.py` and `snapshot.sh` as-is. Add per-game timeouts, the git
    SHA, kept win samples, and structured, never hand-curated, progress data.

15. **Process guardrails the owner had to add:** never idle; after 3 rejects in one area, switch area or take a
    structural swing; track functional areas; archive one replay per iteration in the same commit; run benchmark tallies
    on a schedule; don't count neutral accepts as progress; keep charts and documents current. Each owner intervention
    in 2022 corrected toward bolder swings, external measurement, or the basics (symmetry, bytecode). Start there.
