## 2. What worked (with the evidence as written)

Ordered roughly by ladder impact, because the project's own late finding is that mirror-gate wins mostly did
**not** show up on the ladder. Ladder numbers are batch Bradley-Terry ratings on the Elo scale with 95% intervals;
note that ratings drift between refits (e.g. g_iter12 was "1823 +- 46" at its submission and "1716 +- 41" in the
final `ELO.md`), so only numbers from the same fit are comparable.

### 2.1 Copying the opponent's winning tactic -- the largest ladder gain of the project
- Owner, PROMPTS 40-41: "whenever the enemy uses a tactic against you, learn to use that tactic against other bots",
  submitted as its own build, with the defence built separately.
- **Iteration 63 (copy poortho's school rush)**: second miner walks to the enemy HQ, plants a design school within
  r2 8, its landscapers bury the HQ. Paired gate **ACCEPT, discordant 60-34 at 192 pairs**. Ladder: **g_iter12
  "240 band games, 111-129, rating 1823 +- 46, rank 13 of 86, field score 80.2% -- +79 over g_iter11 (1744 +- 47 on
  the refit), the first step outside the error bars since the ladder began"**; "53 wins before r1300 against
  g_iter11's 18". An enabling discovery came with it: the released maps all have origin (0,0) ("47 of 47 maps"), so
  the rusher no longer spent 180-500 rounds probing edges (planted r49 instead of r256 on GSF).
- Scorecard honesty: the copied **drone raid** was null twice (arm65 1789 +- 73 vs 1817; arm69 1811 +- 72 = g_iter12),
  poortho's **vaporator economy** copy was REJECT 3-20, the **burst rush** copy of rzhan11 was refuted at the
  diagnostic, and the laurenschneider **enclosure** copy probed 4-44 on the ladder. Copying worked when the copied
  tactic was cheap and self-contained; it failed when the tactic was the output of an economy we did not have.

### 2.2 The rewrite R1 (owner-forced, PROMPTS 48-49) -> g_iter13
- After seven level-or-worse iterations on g_iter12's architecture (81-87), the agent rewrote "lattice-first";
  the first arms were far below (r1s3 1626 +- 80, r1s8 1642 +- 78 vs g_iter12 ~1800), then converged:
  **r1s15 at 240 band games 134-106, 1829 +- 46, +49 over g_iter12 (1780 +- 40), "about 1.6 standard errors, p ~
  0.11"**, accepted as g_iter13; firmed at **1807 +- 39 on 336 (g_iter12 1777 +- 40)**. Later the project found this
  carried a winner's curse: g_iter13's acceptance games rate 1791 +- 47, its later 192 games 1738 +- 52.
- Owner's reaction (PROMPTS 51): "It sounds like the rewrite did the trick to get you unstuck."

### 2.4 Late head-to-head acceptances (g_iter14..g_iter19) -- real mechanisms, invisible on the ladder
Each judged by three alternating 48-game band blocks of candidate and incumbent: R1 stage 17 rescue targeting
(+62 +- 85), R7 shoot the enemy carrier first (+22 +- 85; "survives the assault in all six" exact team4 replays),
R12 home school first on early scout (+27 +- 85), R13 drone supply (+74 +- 86), R17 levee (+44 +- 85), R18 capped C3
drone shell (+82 +- 86). The 2026-09-30 calibration (PROMPTS 80, 192 games vs the 10 bots above + 2 below):
**"six acceptances summing to +311 on their head-to-heads ... +12 on the ladder, all inside one error bar."**
Final standing: **g_iter19 1752 +- 29 over 672 games, 11th of the 65 bots, field score 81.4%, 23.9% vs the 10
higher bots.** Mechanisms like R7 demonstrably fired (`@shoot kind=0` 20-52 times a game in the replays) but the
effect was below the instrument's resolution.

### 2.5 Things that worked as engineering
- **Diagnose before gating**: counters at the decision point caught inert candidates cheaply (39, 46, 49, 83, 85,
  R14, R15 "never fires").
- **Census-driven targeting**: the seat census ("of the 52 losses with a wall and an open tile at r500, 51 have no
  landscaper of ours adjacent") produced the seat walk; the idle-miner census ("895 (47%) mined nothing between
  r300 and r500") produced It.34; the production census produced It.50.
- **Exact replay of field losses (puppet)** made field-only mechanisms testable: "the Constriction loss to
  cormackikkert ... replayed with g_iter13 on side B is IDENTICAL to the recording through r2427 in every
  category". R7 survived 6/6 recorded assaults; R11 won 6 of 14 exact rush replays (but lost the head-to-head).

## 3. What did not work / closed directions

Kinds, as the ledger labels them: refuted, priced below the gate, blocked, does not fire, level.

- **Big structural programs on wrong premises, run too long before a field check.**
  - *Citadel* (It.6, wall at Chebyshev 2 with sealed buildings): ten 24-game sweeps 9,5,6,7,8,6,4,5,4,6 of 24.
    "16 tiles halve the per-tile rate of the same landscapers."
  - *Plateau* (It.18-20 + seven slot sweeps): gates 42-54, 25-39, 15-33; sweeps 2-10/24. Won chosen diagnostics by
    10-15% and lost the random mirror ("Two chosen diagnostics are not a forecast").
  - *Enclosure* (36 stages in one day): closed when its own pre-written criterion was met; ladder probe of the
    archetype **4-44, 1406 +- 165**. Bound by measured geometry ("inflow is holders adjacent to the structure that
    dig an external tile, times half").
  - *Lattice* (cand78, 17 stages): "the right shape and the wrong size -- the economy that would pay for the bodies
    is the thing the bodies protect."
  - *Quarry/pinwheel* (r4s1-s5): 1739-1764 vs g_iter13 1776-1789; "each variant moves wins between opponents and none
    reaches the incumbent."
- **Defences against things the mirror opponent never does**, priced by the mirror: net guns (gate4 REJECT 24-40),
  home-defence drones (It.10 120-120; It.23 16-32), gun perches (31 7-25, 31b 13-35), one gun on a raised site
  (37: 2-14, then paired 0-10), the keeper (47d paired 2-14), the perch (It.24 51-61, ladder 11/48 = baseline).
- **Economy reallocations in the mirror**: 5th miner (gate16 56-72), 8 miners first (It.26 50-62), six early
  miners (refuted at diagnostic, -13% ring), tripled economy (not gated; 4,866-6,165 soup idle), vaporator bank
  650 -> 500 (paired 2-8). "Three measurements now agree ... soup spent on anything but landscapers before the
  school drowns costs the wall race." Note the irony: this mirror-imposed taboo is exactly the post-flood economy
  the field's top bots won with, and the project's final "next lead" is that same economy freeze.
- **Rush defences**: 0 of 5 against a real rusher (62, 64, 66, 71 refuted; 53, 54, 56 refuted); "Killing the spawner
  is the wrong answer to a spawner that is rebuilt for 150". R2 soup-free defence ACCEPTED paired vs arch_rush
  (15-3) but **withdrawn on the ladder (1741 +- 46 vs 1798)**: its squatter rule cost against lift-and-bury bots.
- **Ladder arms of 96 games**: "three times now (cand87, r1s13, r1s14) a 96-game arm read +18 to +26 and fell back
  at 240". cand81s8 arm read +47 at 96 games and closed at 1783 +- 48 vs 1814 at 240 ("the arm's +47 was noise").
- **Self-play proxies for the ladder**: the league backtest (9 builds x 11 opponents x 8 cells, 792 games):
  Spearman 0.85 with the ladder but "the five builds at 1735-1807 read 85.2-86.4%"; six of eleven opponents saturated.
  The **puppet league** (open-loop replays of our losses as opponents) is "INVALID as a strength measure ... it
  rewards difference, not strength" (g_iter12 vs g_iter13 36-1 discordant).
- **Archetypes that do not reproduce the field**: `arch_drone` never reached the ring; `arch_raider` "cannot land
  cargo against a shooting HQ"; "The field raids with 100-400 drones, and no archetype of ours reproduces that."
  Exception: `arch_rush2` reproduced poortho's kill (g_iter11 dead at r166 vs poortho r130).

## 4. Method lessons

### 4.1 The loop (TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md, year-agnostic)
- Three instruments, never confused: **mirror gate** (is this change better than what it replaces), **archetype
  spars / puppet** (does it survive a specific attack the mirror never mounts), **scrimmage ladder** (are we stronger
  vs other people's code). "The gate decides; the ladder is the consequence" -- this ordering was **reversed** by the
  end: from 2026-09-29 the ladder head-to-head is the judge and the mirror "stays a crash and regression screen".
- Phase 0 before strategy: rules digest tagged with engine source lines; engine built from source; determinism
  check; replay reader; bytecode monitor; snapshot/mirror tools; unit tests; charts. Iteration 0 = smallest legal bot
  pushed through every instrument.
- Funnel: Read (trace the motivating replay; enumerate mechanisms) -> Pre-register (mechanism, decision-point
  counter, reachability, trigger frequency, price, falsifier, dose ladder with a byte-identical zero arm) ->
  Diagnose (one logged game; "No test starts until the mechanism demonstrably fires") -> Gate -> Submit.
- Candidate sources in rotation: own-replay degeneracy; block census (correlation + onset); capability gaps.
  "After three consecutive rejects in one area the next candidate must come from another area."
- Stall protocol: ablate -> API sweep -> re-read games -> RESEARCH.md -> structural attempt -> rewrite.

### 4.2 Gating and statistics -- what was learned, in order of discovery
1. **Fixed seed = repeated games.** The 2020 engine seeded robot IDs and sandboxed `Random` from the map file, so a
   pairing on the same map and side replayed identically: "491 of 2,825 ladder games were exact repeats ... a
   240-game mirror gate held at most 104 distinct games". Fix: patch `LiveMap.getSeed` to honour
   `-Dbc.game.seed`, seed every game, record the seed, dedupe `(teamA, teamB, map, seed)` in the fit.
2. **Control run** to test the harness itself after six rejects in a row: `control1`, identical copy vs incumbent,
   **49-47 after 96 games** -- the harness was fair.
3. **Paired mirror.** Under a fixed seed the candidate replays the incumbent's exact game wherever its change does not
   fire, so an unpaired mirror "scores the draw": gate 40c read 34-46 REJECT "when 74 of its 80 pairs were concordant
   and the rest 4-2 for the candidate". Each cell is played candidate-vs-incumbent and incumbent-vs-itself on the same
   seed; SPRT (H0 0.50 vs H1 0.58, alpha = beta = 0.05) on discordant pairs only; batches of 16 pairs, cap 320.
4. **Cap rule** (because the p1 = 0.58 bound needs ~20-0 on discordant pairs): at the cap, sign test p < 0.01 with at
   least 12 discordant pairs accepts/rejects; p < 0.10 keeps the change "provisional" for stacking. Edge cases were
   uncomfortable: "42c 12-2 kept, 49b 9-0 not" -- and the agent correctly refused to rewrite the rule after the count.
5. **Read diagnostics against a control** (incumbent vs itself, same map/side/seed), never the other side of the same
   game: "the side effect on one map is 7-18% of the ring ... larger than most candidates' effects".
6. **Mirror gains do not transfer**: "a mirror result of 64.6% became two and a half points against the field ...
   what the mirror rewards is what the incumbent lacks, and the field lacks less of it." Gate g13 made it explicit:
   **SPRT REJECT 24-39 (LLR -3.24) for g_iter13 against g_iter12 while the ladder had g_iter13 +30 on 336 games each.**
   (The project cites its own 2025 METHODS §8b: a self-play dose ladder overstates mechanisms that exploit our own
   architecture's failings and understates those that matter only against dissimilar opponents.)
7. **Two-arm rule for archetype-specific changes**: paired vs the archetype must ACCEPT and the plain paired mirror must
   not REJECT (It.58: 28-6 and 2-2).
8. **Winner's curse on the incumbent**: compare a candidate with **control blocks of the incumbent played in the same
   period (alternating blocks, same pool)**, never with the incumbent's pooled history.
9. **Then the winner's curse one level up**: head-to-heads of 144 a side carry +-85; accepting any positive point
   estimate "accepts noise about half the time". Final rule: the first head-to-head must reach **+43 (one SE)**,
   a second head-to-head confirms, and acceptance needs **the pooled 288-a-side fit's lower one-SE bound above zero**.
10. "Unpaired 30-game records against one bot measure the map draw and the opponent's economy more than the build."
11. Never read a batch until complete ("games ending early are the losses (or the wins)").

### 4.3 Ladder / Elo design
- Started with sequential K=32 Elo and one shared "us" rating; broke badly: **"96 calibration games against unplaced
  bots (88 wins) lifted us from rank 65 to rank 4 of 66, above bots 104-9 against us."**
- The "just above us" / challenge pool drifted to bots beating us 70-99%, where 48-game blocks move "4-14 wins on
  noise" and most games were locked from review (owner, PROMPTS 8: "Is it possible you're working on beating a roster
  that's too strong at the moment?").
- Final design (owner-approved, PROMPTS 15-17): **batch Bradley-Terry fit by minorise-maximise over every game**, Elo
  scale, weak prior (one virtual win and loss vs a 1500 anchor), **each build its own player**, standard errors from
  Fisher information; **band pool = the 8 rated bots nearest the build on either side**; **calibration blocks of
  never-played bots, two games each** (all 65 ladder bots placed in one 96-game block, 88/96); a build's grade =
  rating +- 95%, rank, **field score** (expected score vs every ladder bot one game each), and later "field score vs
  higher-rated bots" (PROMPTS 53). Raw win rates are never compared across builds.
- Withdrawal rule: withdraw if the upper end of the 95% interval falls below the previous submission's rating
  (g_iter4 withdrawn: 46/228, Wilson upper 25.6% < g_iter3's 25.9% -- under the old win-rate rule).
- An unrated candidate's pool must centre on the **incumbent**, not the build of our latest game (arm81 block 1 went
  45-3 against a pool 400 points weak, centred on the enclosure probe at 1406).
- Ladder games are cheap in 2020 (~1 CPU-minute each; "65 scrimmages took 8 minutes on the 8-core VM at 6 in
  parallel"; "A 240-game gate is under an hour"). The owner pushed for volume (PROMPTS 10-11: "play more games to
  determine what the true ladder grade is") and later for a calibration run (PROMPTS 80).

### 4.4 Process rules
- External bots: never read source (compiled by a tool that prints only counts); played only as scrimmages (random map,
  random side, rotating band opponents); their stdout silenced; external bots never play each other.
- Replay review of external games: initially only at >= 20% win rate ("locked" tier, enforced fail-closed by
  `tier-check.sh`); **retired by the owner (PROMPTS 45)** -- "the ladder works better for that anyways". Its first use
  read ronniesong0809's lattice economy, which reshaped the program.
- Puppet rule (PROMPTS 64): "it's OK for us to 'download' a scrimmage game, and examine it, and we can use it to play
  our own internal bots against each other, but in an actual contest there would be no mechanism to play a benchmark
  opponent using the puppet-replay." Puppet games never enter `games.csv` (`scrim-record.py` refuses `pup_` labels).
- Every user prompt recorded verbatim; push after every commit; unit tests after every bot or tool change; "Nothing
  stale stays in the repository"; docs are the owner's dashboard (PROMPTS 20: "I frequently look at the docs in the
  Github repository to get a sense of where we are").
- Standing cron: `/loop 30m task check. If the VM is idle and nothing is in the workqueue, start a new idea.`
  PROMPTS 36-37: "Please don't wait until the next task check to start new work ... The loop task check is a failsafe."
- All times in owner's PDT (PROMPTS 50); run ids stay UTC machine stamps.

### 4.6 How the agent went wrong (self-documented)
- Treated the mirror as the judge for a week; accepted mirror wins (55-90%) that moved the ladder 0-25 points;
  rejected field-facing ideas because the mirror priced their cost but could not see their benefit.
- Fought the economy symptom with the wrong instrument: three measurements in the mirror said "any pre-flood soup
  diversion costs the wall race", while every top bot ran a large pre-flood vaporator economy. The last HANDOFF "next
  lead" is still the post-flood economy freeze: "Median at r1000: 0 miners, 0 schools, 0 centers, while the bank piles
  up unspent".
- Ran 36-stage, 17-stage and 7-sweep programs on a single map trio before any field check.
- Asked the owner for decisions the loop should make (PROMPTS 44, 48).
- Built archetypes that did not reproduce the field's attacks, then could not test defences (rule 5 blocked tests).
- Used sub-agent "design panels" (four designers, three adversarial judges, a synthesis) -- their predictions were
  modest (+5 to +25 Elo, 5-30% success) and the owner questioned their latency.
- Instrument bugs: every instrument "was wrong on first use" (RESEARCH.md), and here too: sequential Elo, fixed seed,
  unpaired mirror, control-free diagnostics, wrong-pool arms, puppet league.

## 5. Bot architecture and the basics (with 2023 mapping)

Files are `src/bot/*.java` (Java 8, package `bot`, ~2,170 lines of dense code). Accepted builds frozen by
`tools/snapshot.sh` (rewrites `package bot;`).

### 5.1 Structure
- `RobotPlayer.run` -> switch on `rc.getType()` -> one `Robot` subclass per type -> `Robot.loop()`.
- `Robot` (abstract): turn loop + bytecode monitor, per-robot LCG RNG seeded from the id (no `Math.random`, for
  determinism), a **per-turn sensing cache** (`sense()`: one `senseNearbyRobots()` bucketed into `enemies[64]`,
  `friends[64]`, nearest enemy, our HQ info), shared helpers `tryMove`, `tryBuild` (relative tie-break toward a
  target, never compass order), `fleeFrom`, `climb`, `shootDrone` (ranked targeting).
- `C.java`: every tunable constant in one place, each annotated with the measurement that set it.
- `Debug.log("@tag k=v ...")` once per decision so that "a mechanism's firing is a grep count"; kept trivial
  because "the sandbox rejects reflection-flavoured calls ... a rejected Debug class takes the whole bot down".
- **2023 mapping**: port the skeleton as-is (type switch, base class with loop/monitor/cache, constants file,
  @tag logging). In 2023, logs go to indicator strings and System.out; check where System.out lands in .bc23
  replays *(inference: verify the 2023 replay keeps robot stdout/log lines)*.

### 5.2 Bytecode
- `Robot.loop()`: record `rc.getRoundNum()` before `turn()`; after, `Clock.getBytecodeNum()`; overrun if the round
  changed; near-miss at 90% of the type limit; print `@bc t= used= max= near= over=` every 100 turns and on every
  overrun; replay dumper's `--bytecode` cross-checks the engine's per-robot bytecode field.
- Techniques that mattered: allocation-free symmetry observation ("the object version cost ~245 bytecodes a tile and
  put miners over their budget 120 times in one game"); strided sampling of `senseNearbySoup` (`Miner.rememberSoup`:
  at most 12 tiles per scan, only on alternate rounds, skipped above 4,500 bytecodes); `@bcprof` section timing when a
  turn exceeds 8,000; bytecode guards on periodic tasks; reading the shared channel only on one residue mod 3.
  "An overrun candidate gates at 50% whatever it is worth."
- **2023 mapping**: steal the monitor verbatim (limits differ by type; carriers and launchers will be the hot
  paths). The strided-scan idea maps to sensing wells/islands/clouds; the residue-scheduling of expensive tasks
  maps to shared-array scans.

### 5.3 Navigation (`Nav.java`)
- Greedy step minimising Chebyshev distance with a Euclidean tie-break, an oscillation guard (last 6 tiles
  forbidden), and **bug wall-following with per-robot random handedness** after a few turns without progress; leave
  bug mode on getting closer than ever. `stalled()` after `STALL` (10) turns without improvement lets callers mark
  a target unreachable. Water-awareness via `legal()`.
- **The most expensive nav bug of the project** (It.50): `Nav.setTarget` resets the stall counter whenever the target
  changes; the miner alternated between a soup target and an explore target every turn, the stall never counted, and
  miners froze with soup in memory ("every miner's mined count identical at r300 and r500"). The one-line fix gated
  **32-9**. Same family: re-picking "the nearest" every turn also reset the stall (fixed earlier with sticky targets).
- Diagnostic: `--navstats` (moves, A-B-A oscillation share, coverage per mille, first enemy-HQ contact).
- **2023 mapping**: currents push units and clouds slow them; walls and wells block. The greedy+bug+stall design
  ports directly; add current-awareness *(inference)*. Unit-test the stall/target-change interaction explicitly.

### 5.4 Economy
- HQ builds `MINERS_EARLY` (4) at once, then replenishes above a reserve; first miner (born round 2) is the
  **builder**; other miners mine nearest remembered soup with a **sticky target**, deposit, explore otherwise.
- Lessons with numbers: deposit only a worthwhile load (It.33: miners next to a drop-off "spends an action on every
  7 soup: 13-22 deposit actions per 30 mining actions", field "one deposit per 14 mining actions"); a stalled full
  miner builds a drop-off where it stands (It.34b); remove reserves no one spends (It.43b: "the bank ended every game
  at 2,000-12,000 unspent"; 30-8); the onset table's earliest predictors are economic -- "net worth (us-them) at
  r150 (+0.44) and mines, robots, spawned at r200 (+0.40 to +0.52)" over 786 games.
- Spawn tiles: "The eight tiles around a spawner are infrastructure" -- parked units, buildings and dug pits silently
  stopped production (Constriction: "two landscapers all game"; school boxed in, It.11; "the doorstep" It.42c).
- The decisive gap vs the top bots: they converted the same mined soup into 20-65 vaporators (permanent income)
  and hundreds of drones; "we mine as much as the field's lattice bots ... the difference is where the soup goes".
- **2023 mapping** *(inference)*: carriers ~ miners; HQ ~ the only drop-off (no refinery). Port: sticky well
  targets, stall -> mark unreachable, full-load trips weighed against 2023's load-dependent move cooldown, a
  per-round "idle resources at HQ" metric (spend it), a spawn-congestion census around HQs, and an onset table of
  Ad/Mn mined by r100-300. The 2020 meta-lesson -- invest in permanent income early and convert it to fighters --
  maps to well saturation and mana-to-launchers in 2023.

### 5.5 Exploration
- 8x8 **sector exploration**: nearest sector neither seen nor given up (`MapState.nextSector`, salted tie-break by
  id); a sector is marked bad when the walk to it stalls. Raised coverage 22.8% -> 37.5% on WateredDown (It.1).
- When the origin is unknown, explore along rays rather than random points (It.52; mechanism fired, gate 52-53 neutral).
- **2023 mapping**: same sector scheme on the 0-based 2023 grid *(inference: 2023 has no random origin offset;
  verify)*; seed with symmetric images of our HQs and wells.

### 5.6 Symmetry (`MapState.java`)
- Three hypotheses as a bitmask (rotation, mirror-x, mirror-y). Pruned by (a) terrain: every observed tile's
  elevation is compared with the remembered value at its image, (b) **`pruneEmpty`**: a robot that can sense the
  current enemy-HQ guess and sees no HQ kills that hypothesis, (c) sighting the enemy HQ keeps only consistent
  hypotheses. Contradiction resets to all three. Unit-tested (`test/bot/MapStateTest.java`).
- Lessons: "A symmetry guess must be looked at before anything gathers around it. Raiders rallied for 1,500 rounds
  beside the rotation image of our HQ; the real one was 20 tiles away." "Robots born inside a sealed area know
  nothing ... whoever learns a map fact must post it once; the HQ must re-post it" (It.7/8 piece of the 39-9 accept).
- **2023 mapping**: steal directly (2023 maps are rotation or reflection). With 1-4 HQs per team, check every own HQ's
  image; prune with walls/wells/islands/clouds/currents seen.

### 5.7 Communication (`Comms.java`)
- 2020: 7-int messages on a shared, public, fee-ordered chain readable one round late. Word 6 is an
  **authenticator** (team- and round-salted hash), `ours()` checks it; HQ re-posts `HQ_LOC`, `MAP_ORIGIN`,
  `ENEMY_HQ` every 100 rounds; newborns scan 12-25 blocks back; announcements are repeated "three rounds running
  because readers see one block in three"; roles are named by "the one robot that knows the count" (the school names
  masons/lattice workers by id) rather than self-elected (self-election gave "16-18 masons a game").
- Bug worth remembering: `Robot.loop` called `init()` before setting `round`, so a newborn's backward scan read
  nothing; found only after four diagnostic rounds (It.36d/e).
- **2023 mapping** *(inference)*: the 64x16-bit array is team-private persistent state, so authentication is
  unnecessary, but the lessons transfer: define ownership of each slot and what happens when its writer dies or
  stops being in write range; have the HQ refresh critical facts; let a single authority assign roles; test that
  newborns read state on their first turn. Writes only near HQ/amplifier/island make "who can post a fact" a design
  constraint, as it was for sealed robots in 2020.

### 5.8 Combat and micro
- 2020 combat was drones lifting units and net guns/HQ shooting; few transferable micro patterns. Portable ones:
  - **Rank targets by consequence, not distance.** The HQ "fired 2,340 shots in the assaults and none at a loaded
    carrier" because an empty drone beside our units outranked the enemy carrier; re-ranking (R7, `Robot.shootDrone`
    ranks k=0..4) survived 6/6 exact replays of the assault.
  - **Arrive as a wave, not a trickle**: "drones arriving one at a time die one at a time (25 built, 3 pickups)".
  - **Respect the defender's turn order and range**: "The HQ ... acts before our units each round. A drone that ends
    its move inside 15 is dead before its pickup cooldown clears; a diagonal step from r2 25 lands at 13."
  - **Timed assaults**: the strong bots attacked on clocks (team4 r1551-1603, benzyx r1217/r1615, cormackikkert
    r2103); loss-end-round histograms exposed them.
- **2023 mapping** *(inference)*: launcher micro (attack r2 16, vision r2 20, cooldown) needs its own kite/strike
  design; take the targeting-by-consequence rule (e.g. carriers carrying anchors, launchers that can shoot this
  turn), wave assembly before engaging, and loss-class histograms by end round.

### 5.9 Code quality observations
- Dense one-line style with long inline rationale comments citing iterations and measurements -- excellent provenance,
  poor readability; `Robot.java` accumulated lattice/quarry helpers that are dead in g_iter19 (`isLot` returns false,
  `isQuarry` returns false). Expect the same drift in 2023 unless pruned.
- Tests are thin for the bot (`CommsTest`, `MapStateTest`, `NavTest`) and thicker for tools (`test_tools.py`,
  `test_metrics.py`, the puppet's golden fixture).

## 7. Pitfalls and gotchas (with cost when stated)

1. **Fixed engine seed**: 491 of 2,825 ladder games were exact repeats; every gate before 2026-09-24 overconfident
   ("gate29b's 160 games held 83 distinct cells"). Needed an engine patch.
2. **Unpaired mirror read as a result**: gate 40c 34-46 REJECT was 74/80 concordant draws; four rounds of It.36
   diagnostics read side effects (7-18%) as signal.
3. **Disk full, repeatedly**: driver filled during a block collection (2,451 loss replays deleted); VM 20 GB at 100%
   (block lost its results table; 7.2 GB freed); driver 99% (2.6 GB pruned); "the 2021 project's 8.3 GB removed
   with the owner's leave"; VM filled with 129 per-game class trees from `pair-batch`. `gauntlet/` is 7.3 GB now.
4. **Shared class trees**: a second run recompiling a tree in use made a block "silently [play] a different bot"
   (predecessor); block 49 lost; `run-dev.sh` collisions lost "six games of twenty in broadr1".
5. **`vm-sync.sh` used `rm -rf src tools ... && tar -x`**: launching a run during gate81 cost "about ten cells".
6. **Two launches of the same gate** (a waiter and a manual start): both died, gate 10's first attempt aborted at 9-23.
7. **Background loops in a tool call die with its shell**: block 46's study "silently skipped".
8. **Hung games are invisible**: a diagnostic "ran 83 minutes with no replay and no log" (predecessor) -> `GAME_TIMEOUT`.
9. **Editing a running bash script** corrupts it (predecessor lost a finished 450-game tournament's collation) ->
   re-exec from a private copy.
10. `pgrep -c` counts two processes per game; never `pkill -f` a pattern in your own command line.
11. **Wrong pool for a candidate arm** (centred on the last-played build): arm81 block 1 went 45-3; block discarded.
12. **Unposted finished blocks** found only at shutdown (two r4s4 blocks).
13. **Docs inconsistent with data** (PROMPTS 19), wrong chart dates (PROMPTS 46), timezone mix (PROMPTS 50).
14. **Ordering bugs in the bot**: `init()` before `round` set (newborn scans read nothing); nav stall reset on
    target change (miners froze for months of game time -- fix worth 32-9); "a fixed list of stations needs a stall
    exit" (It.5: "six of sixteen landscapers with 0 digs", gate 8-24); claim races in "nearest free tile" rules.
15. **Single-map diagnostics hide map classes**: It.5's diagnostic map "has room for all eight posts"; edge and corner
    HQs broke it. "Diagnose on an edge HQ too, and count idle units per role."
16. **96-game arms** (+-72) read noise as findings three times; 30-game per-opponent records measure the map draw.
17. **Self-play-strong archetypes are not field-strong**: arch_rush 1699 / arch_rush2 1674 on the ladder while beating
    our builds 22-26 of 48.
18. **Sub-agent workflows were slow** relative to their value (PROMPTS 71-72).
19. **Sandbox**: reflection-flavoured calls (getClass, stack traces) get a class rejected at instrumentation, which
    takes the whole bot down at spawn; exceptions cost 500 bytecodes.

## 8. Top 15 takeaways for the 2023 project (ranked by expected value)

1. **Make the ladder head-to-head the judge from day one, with enough power.** Alternate candidate and incumbent
   control blocks in the same period and pool; require a first result >= +1 SE, a confirming repeat, and a pooled
   lower-1-SE bound above zero. bc20 accepted six builds summing +311 that moved the ladder +12.
2. **Copy what beats you, as its own submission** -- the poortho rush copy gave +79, the project's largest gain.
   Prefer cheap, self-contained tactics (early aggression, denial); copies that need the opponent's economy failed.
3. **Phase 0 determinism check, then the paired gate.** Verify that 3.0.15 replays identically under a fixed seed and
   that a per-game seed can be set (bc20 had to patch the engine). If yes, gate on discordant pairs only and dedupe
   (teams, map, seed) everywhere. If not, budget for unpaired noise.
4. **Use the mirror as a regression screen only.** It priced costs and never saw field benefits (g_iter7 64.6% mirror
   -> +2.5 points; gate g13 REJECT vs ladder +30). Never let it veto a field-facing idea.
5. **Batch Bradley-Terry ladder, each build its own player, band pool of 8 nearest, calibrate unplaced bots two games
   each** -- steal `elolib.py`/`elo.py`/`scrim.sh`/`post-block.sh`/`field-score.py` nearly verbatim.
6. **Fix the economy first and keep it running all game.** The earliest onset predictors were net worth and mining by
   r150-200; the project's binding weakness to the end was an economy that froze while "the bank piles up unspent";
   the top bots converted the same income into permanent producers and an army. Track idle resources and production
   per 100 rounds from iteration 1.
7. **Build the replay microscope for .bc23 in Phase 0** (rewrite `ReplayDump.java` on the 2023 schema, keep its
   flags), and the census pipeline (`scrim-study` -> `correlate` -> `onset`, merged per build). Act on early onsets.
8. **Count the decision, diagnose against a control, then test.** One logged game with grep-able `@tags`, compared with
   the incumbent playing itself on the same map/side/seed; many bc20 candidates were inert and only counters showed it.
9. **Audit stalls and deadlocks systematically**: navigation stall counters vs target changes, fixed station lists
   without exits, claim races, spawn tiles blocked by our own units. These repairs were the most reliable accepts.
10. **Kill criteria for big programs, set at the start, plus an early field probe.** bc20 spent days on citadel,
    plateau, enclosure (36 stages), lattice (17), quarry -- all closed level or worse; the enclosure's 4-44 ladder
    probe could have come on day one of the program.
11. **Ladder arms need >= 240 games (or the head-to-head)**: 96-game arms read +18..+47 and fell back.
12. **Build a replay-puppet for 2023 for diagnostics** -- exact reproduction of a recorded field situation made
    field-only fixes testable (R7: 6/6 assaults survived) -- but never use it, or a weak archetype league, as a
    strength measure.
13. **Symmetry and shared facts**: three-hypothesis bitmask pruned by terrain and by "the image is in sight and empty";
    look at a guess before massing on it; one authority re-posts critical facts; newborns read state on turn one.
14. **Bytecode monitor in the loop from line one** (overrun = round changed; near-miss at 90%), allocation-free hot
    paths, strided scans, residue scheduling of expensive reads; zero overruns tolerated.
15. **Infrastructure hygiene**: private class tree per run, atomic VM sync, re-exec runner copies, per-game timeouts,
    prune replays after every posted block (VM disk is 20 GB and shared), one `post-block.sh` command, record every
    seed, never read partial batches, keep docs consistent with data because the owner reads them -- and decide
    without asking (PROMPTS 44, 48).
