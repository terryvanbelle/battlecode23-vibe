## 1. Scope

### What the BC26 project was

A practice entry for Battlecode 2026 run as an autonomous Claude Code loop on a GCE driver VM, from
2026-09-01 to 2026-09-06 (first and last `vs_old_bots_history.csv` rows; the only git commit is
"Iteration 252: screened, not decided - pausing project at g_iter34"). It logged **252 iterations**
and accepted **34 snapshots** (`src/g_iter1` .. `src/g_iter34`). The final bot is one
1531-line file, `src/bot/RobotPlayer.java` (86 KB), byte-identical in logic to `src/g_iter34/`.
Final standing: **10/162 against the benchmark suite**. Against the frozen-snapshot roster at g_iter34:
54/54 vs g_iter1, 53/54 vs g_iter6, 54/54 vs g_iter11, 53/54 vs g_iter16, 49/54 vs g_iter21,
44/54 vs g_iter26, 40/54 vs g_iter31.

BC26 mechanics needed to read the lessons: two teams of rats (Baby Rats + 1..5 Rat Kings), NPC cats
(4000 HP, deterministic AI, fixed waypoints), cheese economy (King upkeep 2/round, starving King takes
10/round), a cooperation mode that any bite/kidnap/sprung trap flips irreversibly into "backstab" mode,
scores as **shares** of cat damage / living kings / cheese transferred. All a team's Kings dead = instant
loss. 90-degree vision cones for Baby Rats; only Rat Kings may write the 64-slot shared array; rats talk
only by `squeak()`. Java 21, engine 1.2.x.

## 2. What worked (with evidence and effect size)

The BC26 project's own conclusion (TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md, "Closed directions"): **"The three changes that
ever paid share one property: capability preserved at ZERO marginal cost -- the trap ring (standing defence,
no actions once placed), the emergency override (spends cheese already hoarded), the movement fix (removes
pure waste). On this record, a proposal that spends anything to gain something has roughly a one-in-fifteen
chance."** The ablation program (below) adds the two largest: the exploration-heading reassignment and the
replacement reserve. Items are ordered by measured size.

### 2.1 Exploration-heading reassignment (Iteration 32) — the single most valuable feature, ~+28 points

- Problem, owner-reported: "Baby Rats tend to get stuck in one small region". Traced rat `id11086` on `rift`:
  walked to the map corner by round ~15, then oscillated for ~1985 rounds, never carried cheese.
- Cause: each rat had a fixed ID-based heading (`rc.getID() % 8`) it always returned to; a heading pointing at
  a nearby wall is permanently wrong. Bonus bug: `Direction.allDirections()` includes `CENTER`, so ~1 in 9 rats got
  a no-op heading.
- Fix: a dedicated explore-call position history; two confirmed explore stalls reassign the heading randomly.
  First version reused the shared stuck counter and regressed (23/40, 57.5%) because benign delivery jams near
  the King reassigned good headings; the dedicated history fixed it.
- Result: 30/40 (75.0%) vs 70.0% baseline. **Ablation on the mirror (Iteration 85): 12/54 = 22.2% without it**
  — "the largest effect measured all session".
- Code: `explore()` in `src/bot/RobotPlayer.java` (lines ~1460-1511).

### 2.2 Cost-aware replacement reserve (Iterations 38-39, re-denominated in 251) — ~+24 points

- Measurement that unlocked it: in a winning replay the King built all 25 rats in rounds 1-25 then never again.
  Six earlier throttle attempts (28-31, 34, 37) had all damaged that burst. "Two measurements replaced seven guesses."
- Iteration 38: refresh the build budget every 400 rounds. Broke `minimaze`/`pipes` by bankruptcy: rat cost is
  `10 + 10*floor(pop/4)`, so a refill burst at population ~19 spent the treasury 1660 -> 150.
- Iteration 39: replacement windows need `REPLACEMENT_RESERVE = 1000` of surplus; the opening burst still spends
  to `RESERVE = 150`. Peers 36/40 (90.0%), "six losses fixed, zero new"; vs g_iter11 70% -> 90%.
- **Ablation (Iteration 87): 14/54 = 25.9% without it.** Later shown to be "the anti-starvation floor": six
  iterations that tried to spend through it all lost (111-114, 120, 125).
- Iteration 251 fixed the unit: `REPLACEMENT_RESERVE = RESERVE_RATS * rc.getCurrentRatCost()` with
  RESERVE_RATS = 8, because a fixed 1000 "hoards 33.3 rats' worth" at 8 live rats and 12.5 at 28. Dose curve
  (peers games / swept-wins): fixed 158/52, 14x 159/57, **8x 162/60**, 4x 159/58. Head-to-head 29/54 (53.7%),
  benchmarks 9 -> 10/162. Accepted as g_iter34.

### 2.3 Turn-before-step movement (Iteration 151) — +18 peer games, the largest mirror result

- BC26-specific mechanic: a rat moving off its facing pays strafe cooldown 18 instead of 10. Replay `--turns`
  showed **3094 of 7048 moves (43.9%) were strafes**; every fallback path called `rc.move(d)` raw.
- `stepTo()` checks `canMove`, turns, then `moveForward()`. Same-game measurement: strafes 50.6% -> 0.0%,
  **26% more moves**.
- Mirror vs g_iter24: **75.9% (+3.8 sigma)**. Peers 69/108 -> 87/108 (+18). vs_old_bots 212/270 -> 232/270.
  Benchmarks 8 -> 6/162, which the agent first used to **reject** it; the owner's tie-break rule (below) re-ran
  peers and recovered it; the game-by-game diff showed the -2 was churn (8 flips, 3 gained / 5 lost, two maps
  flipping both ways). "The single largest improvement in this stretch was one I had thrown away."
- 2023 relevance: no facing in 2023, but the general lesson — **measure what the engine actually charges you per
  action, from the replay** — transfers to carrier load-dependent cooldowns, clouds (+20%) and currents.

### 2.4 Emergency build override (Iteration 40) — ~15 peer games (after being written off)

- When the King sees no allied Baby Rat, drop the build bar from REPLACEMENT_RESERVE to RESERVE: a King holding
  1000 cheese with zero army starves defenceless. Predicted the exact three fixed games before the run.
- Its 95.0% headline was stale-opponent inflation (Section 4.3). The mirror said inert (Iteration 84: 26/54 =
  48.1%, "should not be credited"). **The peer audit (Iteration 175): ablated 76/108 -> 61/108, 19 games changed,
  2 gained / 17 lost.** Mirror could not see it because both sides reach the army-wiped state symmetrically.

### 2.5 King rat-trap ring (Iterations 48, 96, 102, gated in 199/208/211) — defence against rushes

- Benchmarks killed the bot in **21-46 rounds** with traps, kidnaps and throws (first 60 rounds: finalist
  27 PlaceTrap / 38 RatNap / 19 ThrowRat; ours 0/0/0). A standing home guard of one third of the army changed
  the outcome by exactly zero rounds.
- Ring of rat traps around the King: still 0/60 wins, but mean survival r33 -> r1250, r21 -> r748, r46 -> r703,
  reaching r2000 against all three. Accepted on a **pre-registered** rule ("if still 0/60 but survival improved
  substantially, treat as mechanistic partial progress").
- Mirror ablation (Iteration 82) scored 57.4% and was accepted; then benchmark early wipes rose 16% -> 26%;
  restoring the ring halved them to 13% (Iteration 96). Final valuation: "removal costs benchmarks -4,
  close-spawn 4/42 -> 0/42 and wipes 14 -> ~42".
- Later refinement: arm the ring only on a **rush signature** (enemy rat within the King's r^2 25 by round 50,
  later 20, or once attacked), because in BC26 a sprung trap marks the **trap's owner** as the backstabber and
  turns a pacifist opponent hostile. Iteration 199: peers 76 -> 80/108, head-to-head 30/54 (56%).
- Constraint found later: King build radius r^2 8 minus its 3x3 body leaves **16 placeable tiles**; the ring
  was already on 15. Building traps faster bought nothing (Iteration 224).

### 2.6 Cat traps (Iterations 128, 204/205, 213) and the "game-ending lever"

- A cat trap is 100 damage for 10 cheese, credited fully to the owner's cat-damage share, and never triggers a
  backstab. Rats lay one only where it fires at once (beside an adjacent cat). Benchmarks 8 -> 10, peers 80 -> 91.
- Second-order discovery: once both sides cat-trap, **the cats die by ~round 177 and the game ends on points at
  that instant** (`GameWorld.checkWin` has an all-cats-dead ending). Iteration 213 removed the round-100 start
  gate: dose gate 100/50/0 -> peers 79/85/87 of 108, head-to-head 35/54 (64.8%). "We are not ending races
  sooner, we are banking more share within the same race."
- **(inference)** 2023 analog: anchors on 75% of islands is an instant-win lever; the team that first exploits
  a game-ending lever changes the shape of every game, and a long-game-tuned bot is then tuned for games that
  no longer happen.

### 2.7 Smaller accepted changes (named for completeness)

- Iteration 1: King reserve 150 + population cap 15 -> `knifefight` death r210 -> r1200; Gauntlet 14/20 vs
  examplefuncsplayer (the wins were later shown to be coin flips at r1310).
- Iteration 3: movement helpers returned `true` even when they did not move, so a rat re-selected an unreachable
  target forever; returning the real result lets priorities fall through to `explore()`.
- Iteration 24: break 2-tile oscillations (position == position two rounds ago, twice) with a seeded
  Fisher-Yates shuffle over 8 directions, **scoped to economic travel only** (unscoped it fired during combat
  and dropped 67.5% -> 65.0%; scoped 70.0%).
- Iteration 35: Bug2 for delivery only. Ablation ~+5.6 (44.4%). g_iter12 beat g_iter11 head-to-head 70-30.
- Iteration 99: the King's attack runs after its build attempt (one action per turn; it had stopped building
  the moment an enemy arrived). Wipes 16% (25/157) -> 14% (22/157), 3 fixed, 0 added.
- Iteration 138: removed the "desperation raid" toward a 180-degree-rotation guess of the enemy King — the guess
  is wrong on 16 of 27 maps.
- Iteration 240: `BUILD_WINDOW_ROUNDS` 400 -> 200 (refresh the same 25-build allowance more often rather than
  raising the cap). Dose (swept maps of 108): 400 -> 54, 300 -> 60, **200 -> 64**, 100 -> 60. Head-to-head
  31/54 (57.4%). "They all raised the ceiling on how many rats may exist at once; this raises how often the
  allowance refreshes."

### 2.8 Infrastructure and method that worked

- **Bare `java` per game** instead of Gradle: ~2 s per game vs ~10-30 s; 6 parallel games on an e2-standard-8.
- **Head-to-head vs the newest snapshot** as primary accept test (Section 4.2).
- **Ablating accepted features** found more real value than invention ("one 54-game run per feature").
- **Swept-map score** (maps won from both sides) to cancel spawn advantage (Section 4.4).
- **Paired check** (`tools/paired-check.sh`): four matches on one maze map and one open map, control re-measured
  every time; "a screen, not a verdict".
- **Exception counting** in every gauntlet, printed above the loss list.

## 4. Method lessons

### 4.1 The loop as designed vs as practised

`TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md` carries BC22's loop: Gauntlet of `2 * B * N` single games (each opponent x map x side),
accept at `WinPct = 60%` of peer games, pick a losing game, hypothesis (max 10), solution (max 10), mechanistic
verification in the replay, an 8-peer reproduction sample, then a full Gauntlet; "never idle"; after 3
consecutive rejects leave the functional area; high-risk structural exploration is a first-class track. Peer
vs benchmark split (benchmark = <30% win rate, excluded from WinPct), retirement at >=80% two Gauntlets in a row.

In practice the 60% WinPct gate was abandoned. By the end the standing accept rule was **"mechanism pass + a
significant gain on EITHER instrument + no statistically significant regression"**, with the head-to-head vs
the newest snapshot run before every accept, peers run on every accept, benchmarks and vs_old_bots as guards.

### 4.2 Instruments ranked by resolution — and representativeness

From `TRAINING_ALGORITHM.md` (added 2026-09-03, "after a day in which the acceptance headline was uninformative in
five of six audited cases, and inverted the sign twice"):

| instrument | matchup | typical win rate | use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `g_iter<latest>` mirror | even | 50% by construction | primary accept test |
| peers | even | ~60-70% | regression check |
| vs_old_bots | lopsided | ~85-90% | direction only |
| benchmarks | lopsided | ~3% | direction only |

- "An instrument pinned near 0% or 100% cannot resolve a few games: at ~3% and ~88% respectively, +/-2 games is
  the noise floor."
- **But resolution is not representativeness.** "The mirror can prove a feature is not paying for itself. It
  cannot prove a feature is unnecessary." The mirror had 0% early wipes in every build, so it could not see the
  trap ring's value. A whole session of four accepts (peers 60.2% -> 71.3%) took benchmarks 5/162 -> 2/162 and
  wipes 16% -> 26%; all four were reverted ("Net bot change: zero").
- Both directions of error happened: Iteration 63 accepted on lopsided instruments (benchmarks +2, vs_old_bots +2)
  then the mirror said 33.3% and it was retracted; Iteration 78 rejected on 4/162 vs 5/162, retested on the mirror
  as Iteration 88 it was +4 games and accepted.
- "A dissenting even instrument is evidence, not an obstacle to explain away." The 9%-vs-59% difference in how
  often benchmark vs peer games reach round 2000 was true, and using it to dismiss a peer regression "is what
  produced the retracted accept".
- Owner rule that resolved conflicts: **"if benchmark and mirror contradict, break the tie with a full peer
  evaluation"**. It caught errors in both directions (151 rescued at +18; 173 rejected at -28 though benchmarks
  moved only 2).

### 4.3 Peer archetypes: what went wrong, three times

- Synthetic peers (`pure_cooperator`, `immediate_defector`, later `opportunistic`, `rusher`) were forks of
  `src/bot` with policy edits. They **went stale three or four times**, silently inflating every number: the
  Iteration 40 "95.0%" re-measured at **62.5%** (pure_cooperator 90% -> 50%). A line-count guard (25%) missed a
  21-22% drift; a date-based guard was added; then the warning was printed at startup and "missed every time"
  because results were read with a grep, so it is now repeated inside the summary.
- Perfectly synced peers measure ~50% by construction: `pure_cooperator` differed by 8 lines; later its two edits
  became no-ops ("`pure_cooperator` IS us"), and all 27 maps were split by side.
- Final structural finding (Iteration 227): **"Every self-derived archetype is either a mirror or saturated"** —
  an edit of our own bot cannot be stronger than us. Only archetypes that differ "EARLY and STRUCTURALLY" resolve.
- Fix that stuck: `tools/resync_archetypes.py` rebuilds each archetype from the **newest accepted snapshot** (never
  from `src/bot`, which may hold an experiment) by applying anchored, exactly-once text edits, with `--check`.

### 4.4 Statistics as used

- **The Gauntlet is deterministic** for identical code (verified Iteration 35, peers at 174, benchmarks at 193).
  "Re-running a marginal result yields zero new information. Vary the size of the mechanism instead." The agent
  wrongly concluded non-determinism twice (early "RNG-cascade noise"; Iteration 251 comparing against a run that
  was actually a rejected candidate).
- **Chaotic churn**: two unrelated changes both scored 29/54 with 20 wins in common and 9 vs 9 discordant —
  "a noise floor of roughly +-9 games out of 54". The accepted g_iter33 change churned 44 games (26:18).
- **Binomial scale**: "binomial sd at p = 7/162, n = 162: 2.59 games; a 3-game drop: 1.2 sigma". Seven of seven
  mechanism-verified changes falling below baseline: "p = 0.008".
- **Flip asymmetry** (gained vs lost among changed games, in sigma) and **coherent dose curves** are the
  discriminators, not raw churn. Iteration 251 table: g_iter33 accept 26/18 flips 1.21 sigma; rejected 250 8/5,
  0.83 sigma.
- **Swept-map score**: per map, won-from-both-sides / lost-from-both / split. pure_cooperator 0/27 swept, 27/27
  split ("zero information content"); immediate_defector 24/27 swept. g_iter32 baseline total 29/81 swept-wins.
- **Coin-flip band**: "9 points-decided losses sit within 1.0 point of a win"; `safelycontained` lost from both
  sides by 115 cheese of 25,755 (0.22%).
- **Dose-response**: "Always measure zero" (Iteration 45: 4 cheese 53.7%, 16 cheese 37.0%, zero 46.3% -> interior
  optimum). "A parameter is only a dose if it changes the condition actually evaluated" (Iteration 70: radius 8 ->
  2 gave 162/162 identical games because the check only looked one tile ahead).
- **Three cheap checks**: arm-to-arm identity (`same == n` means the change never executed; caught 66, 68, 70);
  normalise counters per round (deaths 67 -> 78 was a flat rate, 0.0570 vs 0.0589); correlate timestamps before
  attributing (5 of 78 deaths within 5 rounds of a throw).
- Pre-registering the decision rule before the result (Iteration 48, 63's third branch) prevented post-hoc
  rationalisation of a 0/60 line.
- There was **no Elo or ladder**. `RESEARCH.md`: a scrimmage ladder supplies diversity "roughly calibrated to your
  current ability"; the project had "the calibrated-but-self-referential instrument and the diverse-but-uncalibrated
  one". Progress was tracked as win% vs a frozen roster of every 5th snapshot.

### 4.5 Hypothesis discipline

- **Trace, don't theorise**: the 2026-09-03 accepts all came from looking for a *stall* in per-100-round activity
  and resources ("action counts collapsing to 6-13 for 350 rounds while holding 1271-1581 cheese").
- **Don't sample only losses**: five iterations (217-221) chased a "catDamage deficit" that was selection bias
  (median share in losses between byte-identical bots was 40.4%).
- **In a mirror, inter-team difference is positional, not policy.**
- **Is the branch reachable?** 137, 139, 141 were "correct chains of reasoning about dormant branches": a cheese
  gate at `> 1000` is dead after ~round 150 (treasury ~1098 / 958 / 773 / 553 at rounds 100-400); a `< 150` gate is
  dead outside starvation; `nearestCat != null` is false where cats are out of vision; the cat engage code was
  unreachable because rats nearly always carry cheese and the delivery branch returned first (three iterations
  tuned unreachable code). **"Read the guard, not just the condition."**
- **Measure the shared pool before changing who spends it** (team-wide `CAT_TRAP.maxCount` 10: King 52
  placements / rats 0 vs King 24 / rats 13).
- **The alternative must be worth less for that unit** (the immobile King traps for free; a mobile rat that stops
  to trap dies).
- **Capability audit**: diff the `RobotController` API against `rc.` call sites. Found `becomeRatKing`,
  `carryRat/throwRat`, `squeak` unused at Iteration ~105-165, despite every opponent squeaking 668-2870 times per
  game. "The signal was not a number that moved; it was a number that never moved, beside an opponent's that did."
- **Instrument before inferring**: Iteration 251 logged why the King did not build (`CHEESE 83.5%`, `CAP 8.2%`,
  `BUILT 7.7%`, `NO_SLOT 0.6%`), killing the agent's first hypothesis.
- **Measuring how a resource is spent does not establish that it is scarce** (`king_census.py` docstring).
- **Re-open closed directions only with a new reason** that bears on the stated cause; 2 of 3 re-opens failed, "and
  both failures were worth their run".

### 4.6 Owner (human) interventions and what they corrected

1. "Once an iteration finishes, I'd like you to immediately start the next iteration" (standing, Iteration 2).
2. Pointed out `g_iter1` already existed as a fixed yardstick -> vs_old_bots tracking started at iteration 7

## 5. Bot architecture and basics, mapped onto 2023

All in `src/bot/RobotPlayer.java` (package `bot`, one class, static per-robot state). Heavily commented with
iteration history; the comments are good provenance but make the file hard to read (86 KB for ~600 lines of logic).

### 5.1 Turn loop, error handling, bytecode

- `run()`: `rng = new Random(rc.getID())`; loop: record `roundAtTurnStart`, dispatch by `rc.getType()`, catch
  `GameActionException` and `Exception` (print), `finally { reportBytecodeBudget(); Clock.yield(); }`.
- `reportBytecodeBudget()`: overrun = round number changed during the turn; near-miss = `> 90%` of
  `rc.getType().getBytecodeLimit()`; written to the indicator string; `ReplayDump` greps indicators for `OVERRAN`
  / `near-limit`.
- Measured usage 4.2% median — "~15,000 unused bytecode per rat per turn... the remaining headroom is in decision
  quality per turn".
- **2023 mapping**: copy the pattern as-is (Java 8 compatible). Limits differ (HQ 20000, carrier 12500, others
  10000). Pair it with the gauntlet's exception counter (Section 6), since a caught exception silently ends that
  robot's turn — exactly what happens in 2023 if a unit calls `writeSharedArray` out of range (2023 restricts writes
  to r^2 9 of own HQ, r^2 20 of own amplifier, r^2 4 of own anchored island; BC26's King-only rule is the same
  shape of hazard). **(inference)** Guard every write with `canWriteSharedArray` and queue messages until in range.

### 5.2 Economy (King = 2023 HQ, Baby Rat = 2023 carrier)

- King (`runRatKing`, lines 76-682): writes own location to slots 0/1 as `x+1`, `y+1` (0 = unset). Desperation
  latch (dead code by the end). Flee a cat within d^2 20. Pick up adjacent cheese. Reactive cat trap. Rat-trap ring
  if `!rc.isCooperation()` (after Iteration 211), `TRAPS_PER_BUILD = 2`. Build at the nearest legal tile if
  `cheese - ratCost >= buildReserve && builtCount < MAX_POPULATION`, else dig if no build tile. Attack last.
- Build policy constants (final): `MAX_POPULATION = cheese > 1500 ? 60 : 25`; `BUILD_WINDOW_ROUNDS = 200` with
  `builtCount` reset per window; `RESERVE = 150` for the opening burst; `REPLACEMENT_RESERVE = 8 * ratCost` for
  refills; drop to RESERVE when no allied rat is visible.
- Rat priorities (`runBabyRat`, lines 742-1016): stuck-cycle bookkeeping; home King from shared array, overridden
  by direct sighting; **deliver if carrying any cheese** (Bug2); cat logic; enemy-rat combat only if
  `!rc.isCooperation()`; `collectCheese()` (nearest visible cheese); `explore()`.
- Population read for free: `4 * ((getCurrentRatCost() - 10) / 10)` lower-bounds live rats (BC26-specific).
- **2023 mapping**: HQ costs are fixed (carrier 50 Ad, launcher 45 Mn), so BC26's population-scaled pricing does
  not exist; the transferable lessons are (a) "income first, then population" — never let military spending starve
  the carrier stream; (b) reserves should be denominated in "units of the thing they buy" (for 2023, e.g. an
  anchor's 80 Ad + 80 Mn, or N carriers) **(inference)**; (c) a steady refill cadence beats raising caps;
  (d) the emergency rule "army gone -> spend the hoard" generalises to "no carriers alive -> rebuild carriers
  regardless of other reserves" **(inference)**; (e) measure the actual build cadence and the "why didn't I build"
  reasons from a winning replay before tuning. BC26's raw-cheese slowdown (0.01 x held) is the analog of 2023's
  load-dependent carrier cooldown; BC26 simply delivered any carried cheese immediately, and Iteration 115 "batch
  cheese deliveries" was rejected (I did not read its details).
- Priority-order hazard: the cat-engagement branch was unreachable because the delivery branch returned first.
  In 2023 a carrier's "flee launcher" check must come before "return to HQ" returns **(inference)**.

### 5.3 Navigation

- `moveToward(rc, target, allowStuckEscape, useBugNav)` (lines 1179-1320): greedy direct step; Bug2 only when
  `useBugNav` (delivery to the King — the only **stationary** target). Bug state: target, closest distance,
  rotate direction from `rc.getID() % 2`, rounds following. Start tracing only if the tile ahead is **terrain**
  (`!senseMapInfo(ahead).isPassable()`) — robots are soft obstacles handled by sidestep. Leave the trace only when
  strictly closer than at the hit point. After 16 rounds of fruitless following, reset and flip rotation.
- Development bugs, each found by measurement: Bug2 on moving targets (chase/flee/nearest cheese) thrashes;
  gating the direct move (not just the trace exit) on "closer than ever" traps pushed-back rats forever; tracing
  around friendly robots is worse than a sidestep (recovered 12.5 points when gated on terrain).
- `tryMove(rc, want, allowStuckEscape)` (lines 1371-1402): direct, then left/right sidestep in ID-parity order;
  if `stuckCycles >= 2` and escape allowed, a seeded Fisher-Yates shuffle of 8 directions.
- `stepTo()` (1335-1346): turn then move forward (BC26 facing; irrelevant in 2023).
- `explore()` (1460-1511): ID-based heading, reassigned after two explore stalls, random fallback, dig if stuck.
- No BFS, no map memory, no visited-tile bitmap.
- **2023 mapping**: Bug2 with "terrain-only tracing, robots are soft" and "stationary targets only" is directly
  reusable for carriers going HQ <-> well. Add currents (a current tile pushes you at end of turn) and clouds as
  terrain properties when deciding whether to trace **(inference)**. Keep the two stuck detectors (2-tile
  oscillation; exploration stall) — in BC26 they were worth more than the pathfinder itself (+28 vs +5.6).

### 5.4 Exploration and symmetry

- Exploration = per-robot fixed heading fan-out plus reassignment. No coverage memory.
- **Symmetry was never inferred.** The only use: a 180-degree-rotation guess for the enemy King, wrong on 16 of 27
  maps. Analysis note: the minimum distance over the three candidate symmetries is "badly polluted" (true 51 vs
  estimate 1 on `corridorofdoomanddespair`); a threshold capturing all 22 early wipes fired on 12 maps with 42%
  precision. Conclusion there: trigger on **observation** (an enemy actually arriving), not on round-1 geometry.
- `RESEARCH.md` calls symmetry inference "Battlecode 101" and lists it as untried.
- **2023 mapping**: maps are rotation/reflection symmetric and each team has 1-4 HQs, so the HQ set alone often
  disambiguates the symmetry at round 1 **(inference — check against the 2023 engine/maps)**; terrain, wells and
  islands observed by scouts can eliminate the rest. Enemy HQ positions matter because HQs hit for 4/round within
  r^2 9. This is the most obvious BC26 gap to close early.
- Side-bias audit: seven `d < bestDist` scans over engine-ordered sensing results handed ties to absolute
  coordinates (`findBuildLocation`, `collectCheese`, `pickUpBestNearbyCheese`, `attackNearestHostile`,
  `nearestOfType`, `nearestEnemyRat`, `digTowardOpenSpace` — all still present in the final file). A true mirror
  split 7/10 side A vs 3/10 side B on the 10-map loop set; later full-map mirrors were 27/54 with every map split
  1-1 (the g_iter9 symmetry audit, logged between Iterations 14 and 15) and 16-11 / 11-16 by side (Iteration 222). Randomising ties cost win rate. The untried idea:
  a **consistent** per-robot preference relative to something symmetric (vector to own HQ, or map centre).
  2023 has no engine chirality, so this bug class is fully exposed **(inference)**.

### 5.5 Combat and micro

- Rats: bite an adjacent cat (cheese-boosted bite above 1000 cheese, effectively dormant); engage a cat if
  `allies > 1 || health > 30`, else flee within d^2 8; lay a cat trap beside an adjacent cat; enemy rats only
  after cooperation breaks: attack adjacent, chase within d^2 8 (d^2 20 from round 300).
- King: attack nearest hostile after the build; flee a cat within d^2 20.
- "Damage does not convert" and "kiting inverted" are BC26-specific (unkillable cats, King kills by cats).
  **(inference)** In 2023 launchers are killable and launcher micro is the main fight, so the transferable lesson
  is only the method: check the premise of a ported technique, and measure exchange ratios (BC26 measured 7 kills
  vs 347 losses) before tuning targeting.
- Iteration 99's lesson transfers literally: the unit that produces should not spend its single action on
  fighting at the moment production matters. In 2023 the HQ can build several units per turn and also auto-attacks
  **(inference — verify attack vs build cooldown interaction in 3.0.15)**.

### 5.6 Communication

- Shared array used for 5 slots: King x/y (0/1), desperation flag (2), enemy-King guess (3/4). "slots 2/3/4 are
  free" by Iteration 244. No resource/well/enemy sightings, no symmetry bits.
- Rats could not write (King-only), and squeak was never used until 166.
- **2023 mapping**: 2023 gives 64 x 16-bit and lets any unit write near HQ/amplifier/anchored island. BC26 offers no
  comms design to steal beyond "use a 0 sentinel by storing x+1". The cross-year advice quoted in `RESEARCH.md`
  (record HQs/resources/symmetry bits; pack bits; e.g. 60 longs as a 60x60 bitmap) is the starting point.

## 7. Pitfalls and gotchas that cost time

1. **Stale derived opponents** — inflated readings for Iterations 10-18 and 32-40 (95.0% vs honest 62.5%); a third
   drift slipped past a 25% line-count guard at 21-22%; then the staleness warning fired "on five consecutive runs
   ... and was missed every time" because it was filtered out by grep.
2. **Wrong instrument for the question** — a day of four accepts, all reverted, "Net bot change: zero".
3. **Exceptions silently end turns** — `run()` catches per turn. The Kings-only shared-array write invalidated
   Iteration 80; a muster bug "aborted the King's entire turn from round 200 on"; "162 games were being run with
   nobody looking". Count exceptions in every gauntlet.
4. **Spec PDF errors** — cat vision √17 vs √30; cat scratch cooldown 15 in the PDF vs 30 in the engine after a
   balance patch. Read the engine source.
5. **Rule asymmetries found late** — backstab blamed on the trap's owner; scores are shares, damage accrues per
   point. "This cost real iterations before it was checked."
6. **Dormant code and nested guards** — five accepted features found inert (106, 128, 138, 141, raid), all accepted
   on 1-2 game benchmark moves (<1 sigma).
7. **Version-control slips** — `git add -A` committed untested code; `git stash` silently did nothing for a
   committed change, producing a fake control.
8. **Concurrent replay dumps** shared one remote path: "Six replays dumped in parallel came back as six identical
   copies ... the most convincing possible form of wrong."
9. **Undecoded actions** read as "never happened" (`RemoveTrap`, `UpgradeToRatKing` missing from the dumper).
10. **Actor/victim confusion** in replay events (three in one audit) and team numbering by side (`bot` is team 1 in
    some replays, team 2 in others: "already caused three wrong conclusions").
11. **Thresholds on accumulating quantities** are not event detectors (movement cooldown >= 30 fired on two strafes).
12. **Loss-only sampling** (five iterations on a selection artifact).
13. **Stale quoted controls** (243, 247) and **mismatched map sets** in guard comparisons (199).
14. **Unused capabilities** unnoticed for 100+ iterations (squeak at 165, becomeRatKing at 105).
15. **`Direction.allDirections()` includes CENTER** — 1 in 9 rats had a no-op heading.
16. **Chart/CSV hygiene** — `plot_progress.py` skipped for most of a day; history rows labelled from the directory
    listing rather than the binary that played (four rows removed); a snapshot created without a commit.
17. **Replay archive lapsed** "after `iter12` and roughly ninety iterations went by without a single sample".

## 8. Top 15 takeaways for the 2023 project, ranked by expected value

1. **Hunt degenerate states before tactics.** In BC26 two small failure-mode preventers (heading reassignment +28,
   replacement reserve +24) outweighed the rest of the bot combined, and ~25 tactical iterations in one day all
   failed. For 2023: stuck/oscillating carriers, carriers idling full, HQ hoarding with no carriers, launchers
   parked out of range, units stuck on currents **(inference)**. Trace per-100-round activity and resources for a
   *stall*.
2. **Primary accept test = candidate vs the newest accepted snapshot, both sides, all maps; judge by the
   game-by-game diff and swept maps.** Never accept or reject on a lopsided instrument alone; when instruments
   conflict, run the full even-matchup evaluation (owner rule that rescued the largest single gain).
3. **Resolution is not representativeness.** Keep at least one instrument that actually poses each threat (rush,
   anchor race, island fights) and read a guard counter beside wins. A defensive feature that ablates as "free" on
   the mirror may be load-bearing against real opponents.
4. **Read the engine, not the spec, for every rule a strategy rests on** (write ranges, anchor HP rules, island
   capture, current push order, HQ attack vs build cooldowns, tiebreak order). Vendor the specific 3.0.15 files.
5. **Count thrown exceptions per game and treat any nonzero count as a failed run.** In 2023 the shared-array write
   restriction reproduces BC26's King-only trap exactly; guard with `canWriteSharedArray`.
6. **Determinism: never re-run; dose instead.** Measure zero, expect concave curves with interior optima, check that
   the "dose" changes the evaluated condition, and use flip asymmetry, not churn, to decide. Expect ~±9 games of
   churn per 54 games for near-identical bots.
7. **Reachability check before every hypothesis** (resource gates that the treasury never reaches, vision gates,
   counter gates, outer guards). A correct argument about a dormant branch predicts nothing.
8. **Symmetry inference early** — never done in BC26 and named as the obvious gap. With HQs, wells and islands all
   symmetric, it locates enemy HQs and unseen resources **(inference: verify on 2023 maps)**.
9. **Audit tie-breaks for absolute-order bias and run mirror matches** on every change touching selection; do not
   fix by randomising (cost win rate twice across two projects); prefer consistent, symmetry-relative preferences.
10. **Capability audit on day one**: diff the 2023 `RobotController` API against our call sites (amplifiers,
    anchors, cargo throwing, boosters, destabilizers, currents) so no mechanic waits 100 iterations.
11. **Economy: income first, then army; reserves in units of what they buy; refresh cadence over caps; an
    "army/carriers gone -> spend the hoard" override.** Extract the real build cadence from a winning replay before
    touching thresholds.
12. **Watch for game-ending levers.** In BC26 cat traps made games end at ~r177 and changed what mattered; 2023's
    anchor win is the obvious analog **(inference)**.
13. **Self-derived opponents are mirrors or saturated.** Prefer frozen snapshots (every 5th) for progress and
    external bots near our level for diversity; if archetypes are used, rebuild them mechanically from the newest
    snapshot (`resync_archetypes.py` pattern) and warn loudly when stale.
14. **Tooling hygiene that paid**: re-measure controls (paired check, two map characters), assert reverts with a
    diff, explicit staging, unique per-run temp dirs, decode every action you count, pin decoder constants with
    tests, label results by what actually played.
15. **Spend the bytecode.** BC26 used a 4.2% median and never spent computation on better decisions. 2023 limits
    are lower (launchers 10,000), but a short BFS over the visible area or a better target evaluation is likely
    affordable **(inference)**; monitor overruns from Iteration 0 as BC26 did.
